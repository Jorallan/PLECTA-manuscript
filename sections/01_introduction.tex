\section{\rev{Introduction}}
\label{sec:intro}

\emph{recovering individual filament identity through crossings}, and the paper

\rev{The properties of carbon-nanotube (CNT) sheets, yarns, and composites
depend on the geometry of their bundle networks: length, width, orientation,
curvature, and contact topology influence mechanical and electrical response
\citep{devolder2013cnt,coleman2006small,volkov2010structural,%
ostanin2013distinct,wittmaack2018mesoscopic}. Scanning electron microscopy
(SEM) resolves this projected network, but routine image analysis often stops
at coverage or orientation distributions
\citep{dong2022detecting,imtiaz2023facile}. Instance-resolved analysis instead
requires each visible bundle to remain addressable across gaps, touches, and
crossings.}

\rev{This requirement is distinct from semantic segmentation. U-Net and its
self-configuring variants can identify filament foreground
\citep{ronneberger2015unet,isensee2021nnunet}, but a binary foreground mask does
not encode object identity. A faded segment splits one filament into several
connected components; a crossing joins different filaments into one component.
The ambiguity is greatest in dense networks, where several local continuations
can be geometrically plausible. Compact-object instance priors---bounded blobs,
centre-directed flows, or star-convex shapes---are poorly matched to long open
curves that may overlap \citep{stringer2021cellpose,schmidt2018stardist,%
kirillov2023sam}.}

\rev{Existing methods address complementary parts of the problem. Ridge and
vesselness filters enhance curvilinear signal but do not assign identity across
junctions \citep{steger1998unbiased,frangi1998vesselness,sato1998linefilter}.
FIRE, CT-FIRE, SOAX, FiNTA, and DiameterJ extract fibres or network statistics
in collagen, biopolymer, and fibrous materials
\citep{stein2008fire,bredfeldt2014ctfire,xu2015soax,%
flormann2021finta,hotaling2015diameterj}; methods such as that of
Depriester et al. address fibre separation directly
\citep{depriester2022fibre}. Learned filament-instance approaches include
FibeR-CNN and recurrent pick-and-trace models
\citep{frei2020fiber,liu2023picktrace}. Orientation-aware branch separation
followed by terminus pairing has also been used for microscopy filaments
\citep{liu2019intersection}. These approaches establish the importance of
centreline and continuation evidence, but dense binary masks still require an
explicit answer to which arms share an instance and which crossing pixels may
belong to several instances.}

\rev{CNT-specific workflows increasingly combine semantic segmentation with
algorithmic tracing and transverse intensity profiles
\citep{peters2026fibre}. The present work focuses on the difficult middle step
of that workflow: binary mask to overlap-aware instances. This separation is
deliberate. It allows grouping to be evaluated independently of the upstream
segmenter and prevents width rendering from changing an identity metric. An
optional CycleGAN/U-Net route supplies masks for the SEM application
\citep{ruehle2021workflow,allan2026cntsem}, but it is not the principal
contribution.}

\rev{We introduce PLECTA, a deterministic graph-matching method for dense
filament networks, named after the Latin \emph{plecta}, a braid. PLECTA
decomposes a skeleton into arms, endpoint stubs, and merged junction nodes. It
then alternates exact per-node continuation matching with globally gated gap
matching while tangent and curvature estimates expand from local arms to
assembled chains. Its output is a set of projected 2-D instance layers in which
crossing pixels may be shared.}

\rev{The study asks three questions. First, how accurately does the fixed
method group method-independent skeleton fragments on unseen procedural scenes,
including high-density cases? Second, which active geometric and topological
components account for that performance, and how sensitive is it to mask
degradation? Third, what does the method deliver in a CNT SEM workflow when the
mask is derived manually or by a learned segmenter? Synthetic scenes provide
exact overlap-aware identity; real SEM remains a development case. The intended
claim is therefore a reproducible instance-reconstruction result under the
tested generator, with explicit limits on distribution transfer, width
validation, and 3-D interpretation.}
