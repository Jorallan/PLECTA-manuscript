\section{Measures}
\label{app:measures}

This section defines every measure reported in the article, together with
what it rewards and its principal limitation. None of the measures depends on
the rendered width of an instance.

\textbf{Common-fragment set.} All measures below except detection \Fone{} are
computed on a common set of fragments. The mask is skeletonised, spurs of at
most 3\,px are pruned, skeleton pixels with three or more 8-neighbours are
deleted, the remaining 8-connected pieces are labelled, and pieces shorter than
6\,px are discarded, so that each fragment is an unambiguous arm between
crossings. This partition depends on neither the reference nor any prediction,
so every method is evaluated on the same fragments. Each fragment then
receives a reference identity and a predicted identity by the same rule. It is
assigned to the instance whose mask overlaps the largest number of its pixels,
counted on each instance's full mask rather than on a flattened label image so
that overlapping instances retain their pixels, provided that this overlap
covers at least 30\,\% of the fragment. Otherwise it is assigned to the most
frequent nearest instance label within 6\,px, read from a deterministically
flattened label image; failing both, it is treated as a singleton cluster on
both sides, so that every fragment enters the evaluation. Ties are resolved in
favour of the lowest instance identifier and the flattening order is fixed, so
the assignment is deterministic. Because junction pixels are removed before
labelling, these measures do not register whether a crossing pixel is assigned
to one instance or to two, only whether the arms meeting at the crossing are
grouped as in the reference.

\textbf{Sensitivity to the scorer's constants.} The four constants above are
shared with the method (Section~\ref*{M-sec:mm-metrics}). The stored
predictions of all four methods were therefore re-scored under one-at-a-time
perturbations of each constant, on a grid fixed in writing before any result
was computed: spur pruning 2--4\,px, minimum fragment length 4--8\,px, overlap
threshold 0.20--0.50 and fallback distance 3--9\,px. The perturbations affect
scoring only; all stored predictions and configurations predate them.
Table~\ref{tab:scorer-sensitivity} reports PLECTA's mean \Fone{} and its paired
margins over each comparator under every perturbation, over the
\PlectaGreedySceneCount{} degraded comparison scenes. Across the
\PlectaScorerSensitivityComboCount{} combinations, PLECTA's mean \Fone{} ranges
from \PlectaScorerSensitivityFOneLow{} to \PlectaScorerSensitivityFOneHigh{},
and the smallest paired margin over any comparator under any perturbation is
\PlectaScorerSensitivityMarginMin{}. Only the minimum fragment length changes
the level appreciably, and it does so in the same direction for every method,
because shorter fragments add difficult, low-evidence units for all of them.
The other three constants change the mean by less than 0.002, and no
perturbation changes the ranking of the four methods.

\textbf{Common-fragment pairwise \Fone{}} is the primary endpoint. A pair of
fragments is a true positive when both identities agree, a false positive when
the prediction joins fragments of different reference strands, and a false
negative when it separates fragments of the same strand:
\begin{equation}
    P=\frac{\mathrm{TP}}{\mathrm{TP}+\mathrm{FP}},\qquad
    R=\frac{\mathrm{TP}}{\mathrm{TP}+\mathrm{FN}},\qquad
    \Fone{}=\frac{2PR}{P+R}.
    \label{eq:pairwise-f1}
\end{equation}
It is a clustering measure of the Rand family
\citep{rand1971objective,amigo2009comparison,argandacarreras2015crowdsourcing},
invariant to permutation of the instance labels, and it rewards keeping a
strand together across every crossing and gap it passes. Limitations: it is
quadratic in fragmentation, so a strand split into $n$ pieces contributes
$\binom{n}{2}$ pairs and a single missed join in a fragmented mask is
penalised many times; and it weights long strands with many crossings far
more heavily than short ones.

\textbf{Adjusted Rand index} \citep{hubert1985comparing} counts the same pairs,
corrected for chance. The correction matters because, with about a hundred
strands per scene, almost every pair belongs to different strands and the
uncorrected index is close to 1 for any prediction. Limitation: over the
comparison set it never differs from pairwise \Fone{} by more than
\PlectaPanelARIMaxGap{}, so it confirms rather than adds information.

\textbf{Variation of information} \citep{meila2007comparing}, in bits and lower
for better agreement, is the conditional-entropy distance between the two
partitions. Its split component,
$\mathrm{VI}_{\mathrm{split}}=H(\text{prediction}\mid\text{reference})$,
penalises dividing one strand into several, and its merge component,
$\mathrm{VI}_{\mathrm{merge}}=H(\text{reference}\mid\text{prediction})$,
penalises fusing several strands into one; together they distinguish methods
that over-segment from methods that under-segment. Limitation: each component
alone is degenerate, since a method that merges everything never splits and
scores zero on the split component, so the two are always reported together.

\textbf{Detection \Fone{}}, the measure reported by DNAi, matches predicted to
reference \emph{objects} greedily by highest intersection over union, down to
\PlectaDetectionIoU{}, DNAi's own default threshold, with precision computed
over predicted and recall over reference objects. A symmetric placement
tolerance of \PlectaPlacementTolerance{}\,px is applied: a reference pixel
counts as found when the prediction passes within this distance, and vice
versa, and the intersection is taken as the smaller of the two counts. Without
it, two independently traced one-pixel centrelines two pixels apart would not
intersect at all, so a strict overlap would measure placement rather than
shape, a problem also stated as a design consideration of the thin-structure
benchmark FISBe \citep{mais2024fisbe}. The real fields show that the tolerance
corrects rather than inflates the score. On the reference-derived axes, a
skeleton of the annotation itself with no displacement, it changes detection
\Fone{} on B58\_110 from \PlectaDetectionBFiveEightOneOneZeroManualStrict{} to
\PlectaDetectionBFiveEightOneOneZeroManualTolerant{}, whereas on the nnU-Net
axis of the same field it raises it from
\PlectaDetectionBFiveEightOneOneZeroUNetStrict{} to
\PlectaDetectionBFiveEightOneOneZeroUNetTolerant{}; the tolerance therefore
matters only where two readings differ in placement. Because it counts objects,
this is the most lenient measure of a fragmented mask and the least
discriminative measure reported. Limitation: it is blind to an arm swap,
which it scores 1.000 where the pairwise score gives 0.000.

\textbf{Crossing Fidelity} measures junction resolution directly. At every
junction node the reference induces a partition of the incident arms, two arms
being grouped together exactly when the reference assigns them to the same
strand, and the prediction induces another; Crossing Fidelity is the fraction
of crossings at which the two partitions are identical. Nodes are the junction
clusters of the same pruned skeleton, and the arms of a node are the fragments
8-adjacent to it. Only nodes whose arms carry at least two distinct reference
strands are scored, since a node whose arms all belong to one strand is a
skeletonisation artefact and scoring it would reward inaction. It is a
diagnostic introduced for this study rather than an established metric,
although it follows measures that assess correctness at branch points rather
than per pixel \citep{gillette2011diademmetric,reilly2018nri}. Limitations: it
is much less discriminative than the pairwise score, so it is used as a count
of resolved crossings rather than for ranking; and it carries no information
about gaps, so it does not reflect the cost of bridging them. Because it shares
its skeleton and assignment with the pairwise score, it provides a finer view
of the same evidence rather than independent confirmation, and it is always
reported together with its all-unpaired baseline.

\textbf{Join \Fone{}} counts decisions rather than pairs and is used on the
real fields, where the two mask sources fragment differently. A
\emph{decision} is a pair of distinct fragments whose endpoints lie within
\PlectaRealJoinReach{}\,px of each other, the largest gap the method bridges;
reference and prediction each either join the pair or not, and precision,
recall and their harmonic mean are computed over decisions. Because it is
linear rather than quadratic in fragmentation and conditioned on the mask, it
poses comparably difficult questions for both mask sources, and splitting and
merging appear separately, as low recall and low precision respectively.
Limitation: fragment pairs that the mask never brings within reach are not
counted, so the measure does not register a strand missing from the mask
entirely; it is therefore always reported with the number of decisions each
mask poses.

\textbf{Measures computed but not used as endpoints.} GraFT's \emph{filament
matched coverage} asks whether each reference strand is matched one-to-one to
some detection, and on it PLECTA ranks \emph{below} both comparators: at the
highest density of GraFT's own generator, \PlectaRegimeHighPlectaMatched{}
against \PlectaRegimeHighGraFTMatched{} and \PlectaRegimeHighDNAiMatched{}. It
is not used as an endpoint because it measures existence rather than
correctness. A method that outputs more objects than the scene contains has a
candidate for every strand, each method's matched coverage equals the smaller
of one and its output-to-reference object ratio to within
\PlectaRegimeCoverageCountResidual{}, and requiring each detection to cover
half of the axis it claims restores the ranking. \emph{Centreline Dice}
\citep{shit2021cldice} was evaluated as an alternative matching criterion and
not adopted. It tests each side's skeleton against the other's \emph{volume},
which a centreline grouping does not have, so it reduces to the placement test
that the tolerance above is designed to correct. No clDice results are
reported.

\textbf{Validity of a reference.} A defective reference cannot be detected by
any of the measures above: it lowers every method's score slightly without
changing their ranking. A reference is therefore accepted only if, scored as a
prediction of itself, it obtains a perfect score on every measure. Any
shortfall indicates that the reference disagrees with its own fragment
assignment and bounds the score any method could obtain against it.

\subsection{Statistical analysis}

Synthetic-scene means and density strata are reported with 95\,\%
nonparametric bootstrap intervals (20,000 replicates; seed 20,260,810),
resampled with stratification by density; the comparison between clean and
degraded masks resamples paired scene differences within density.
Per-coverage points in the comparison figures are plain means of ten scenes,
without intervals. On the real SEM fields the inferential unit is the field,
and the \PlectaRealCrossingTotal{} reference crossings entering the crossing
measures are a count pooled over six condition-specific evaluations of the
three fields, not a sample size. Component ablations are one-at-a-time
experiments on all \PlectaDevelopmentSceneCount{} development scenes and do
not use the evaluation set.

\section{Comparator configuration}
\label{app:comparators}

Section~\ref*{M-sec:res-comparators} of the article summarises how each
comparator was adapted and at what cost. This section describes the mechanism
behind each adaptation, so that the comparison can be verified.

\textbf{DNAi} clusters junction pixels by single linkage and erases a disk at
each cluster. At its released linkage distance of 10\,px, nearby crossings
chain into one cluster, which at 60\,\% coverage reaches 89\,px across and is
replaced by a single small erasure disk, leaving a whole tangle as one
instance. The released default therefore does not reflect the method's
capability on these scenes. Reducing the distance to 2\,px on development
scenes raised development \Fone{} and reduced the merge component of the
variation of information by about a factor of three, confirming this
mechanism; the tuned configuration is the one reported. Two design choices
account for the remaining difference. DNAi commits to a pairing at every
junction of degree two to four without a cost threshold, so it cannot decline
a continuation in the way a penalised unmatched option allows; and it attempts
no junction above degree four, resolving
\PlectaJunctionDNAiDegreeFivePlus{} of those crossings. These are about a
tenth of all crossings, but they carry \PlectaEvidenceHighDegree{}\,\% of
same-strand fragment pairs on the degraded masks, since a single unresolved
junction separates every pair that spans it.

\textbf{GraFT}'s errors are predominantly splits rather than merges (split
component of the variation of information \PlectaGraFTVISplit{}, merge
component \PlectaGraFTVIMerge{}). This follows from its greedy longest-path
cover, which cannot revise an early decision once the scene forms a single
connected graph, as it does above 30\,\% coverage.

\textbf{SIFNE} enhances the image with an orientation filter transform,
thresholds it by Otsu's method and thins it, and thereafter operates on the
binary skeleton alone. It marks every pixel whose eight-connected neighbourhood
sum reaches three as a crossing, erases each such pixel together with a square
neighbourhood, and pairs the resulting fragment termini within an angular
search fan, bidirectionally and one pairing at a time \citep{zhang2017sifne}.
Since every step after thinning operates on a binary axis, the mask is
supplied as the thinned array, and all subsequent stages run as released. Only
the graphical callbacks were re-implemented, to allow unattended execution,
and the manual-correction stage was not used, since no comparator here
involves manual intervention.

One released default is degenerate at these densities and was re-selected on
development scenes. The erasure neighbourhood is
\PlectaSifneShippedJuncSize{} pixels square and is applied at every pixel whose
eight-connected neighbourhood sum reaches three, so the erased area grows with
the number of crossings: on scenes at 20, 40 and 60\,\% coverage, the
remaining mask is 51, 44 and 31\,\% of the input, and the evidence is removed
before any pairing is attempted. On the sparse single-molecule images for
which the method was designed, the erased fraction is small. Selection
proceeded in two stages, with both grids fixed in advance: first the three
settings that determine how much of the mask is retained, then the linkage
gates at the best setting of the first stage. Only the ten development scenes
were used. The selected configuration reduces the erasure to
\PlectaSifneJuncSize{} pixels square and the search radius to
\PlectaSifneFanR{} pixels, raising development \Fone{} from 0.537 to 0.749 and
Crossing Fidelity from 0.502, below the all-unpaired baseline, to 0.831. On the
comparison set, the same change raises \Fone{} from \PlectaSifneShippedFOne{}
to \PlectaSifneFOne{}; both values are reported.

Inspection of the source code shows that the search-fan angle has no effect at
the selected angular gate, because for any fan wider than sixty degrees the
gate is the stricter of the two tests. The neighbourhood radius used by the
orientation filter, by contrast, remains effective when the mask is supplied
directly, because the same value sets the arclength over which every tip
direction is estimated, which is the only evidence its pairing uses. SIFNE's
crossing excision does not bias the scoring: although the deleted crossing
pixels are never restored, the common-fragment scorer also removes junction
pixels and evaluates only whether the arms meeting there are grouped as in the
reference, and SIFNE links across the excised crossing, so it makes exactly the
decision that the endpoint measures.

\textbf{Basu et al.} has no released implementation: the only code published
by its authors accompanies their earlier centreline-localisation paper, and its
address no longer resolves, with no archived copy available. The method is
therefore reimplemented from its published description and reported as a
reimplementation baseline rather than as an external result. Only its
geometric part is reimplemented: a minimum spanning tree over the centreline
pixels, removal of every bifurcation node, and recombination of two- and
three-segment groups scored by the curvature energy of a fitted smoothing cubic
spline \citep{dierckx1993curve} minus a bond energy, selected by a
set-partitioning integer program that is re-solved until the segment set no
longer changes. This part uses geometry alone and therefore accepts an axis
mask directly. The intensity-based part, a filter bank followed by a reverse
diffusion that moves particles onto intensity ridges, is not reimplemented,
because evaluating a substitute front end would measure that substitute rather
than the published method; this baseline therefore evaluates the recombination
step only.

Its two user parameters, the maximum linking distance and the maximum tangent
mismatch, were selected on the same ten development scenes, by the same
criterion as for the other comparators. The values published for the original
simulated benchmark are replaced by \PlectaBasuDelta{} and \PlectaBasuTheta{}
here; with the published pair, the reimplementation scores 0.076 on these
masks, because a linking distance of two pixels permits almost no
recombination when the gaps in the mask are wider. Both results are recorded.
The original description leaves two further quantities unspecified that affect
the result, the smoothing of the fitted spline and the quadrature of the bond
energy; each was set in the direction favourable to the method, and the choice
is recorded. On Crossing Fidelity the recombination decides junctions almost as
well as the exact matching does, but it loses the pairwise margin in the
assembly built from those decisions, because a pairwise score over fragments
is quadratic in fragmentation, whereas a junction count is not.

\subsection{Paired margins}

Paired against PLECTA over the degraded set, the Crossing Fidelity margins are
\PlectaJunctionGreedyDelta{} [\PlectaJunctionGreedyDeltaCILow{},
\PlectaJunctionGreedyDeltaCIHigh{}] over the greedy baseline,
\PlectaJunctionGraFTDelta{} [\PlectaJunctionGraFTDeltaCILow{},
\PlectaJunctionGraFTDeltaCIHigh{}] over GraFT and \PlectaJunctionDNAiDelta{}
[\PlectaJunctionDNAiDeltaCILow{}, \PlectaJunctionDNAiDeltaCIHigh{}] over DNAi.
On clean axes PLECTA resolves \PlectaJunctionCleanPlectaRate{} of crossings.
Leaving every arm unpaired resolves \PlectaJunctionChance{} of them; this is
the all-unpaired baseline against which the rate is read, a deterministic
policy rather than a chance level. Crossing Fidelity is much less
discriminative than the pairwise score: at 60\,\% coverage it spans
\PlectaJunctionSpreadHigh{} between the best and worst methods, whereas
pairwise \Fone{} spans \PlectaPairwiseSpreadHigh{}. Resolution rates decline
for every method at the highest-degree nodes: pooled over the
\PlectaJunctionCount{} reference crossings of the degraded set, PLECTA
resolves \PlectaJunctionPlectaRate{} exactly, but only
\PlectaJunctionPlectaDegreeFivePlus{} above degree four, where no comparator
is higher.

A restricted comparison with DNAi scores only the evidence that a method
limited to junctions of degree four or less, and unable to bridge gaps, can
reach. It changes the clean-axis difference from \PlectaDNAiCleanDeltaFOne{}
to \PlectaDNAiRestrictedCleanDeltaFOne{}
[\PlectaDNAiRestrictedCleanDeltaCILow{},
\PlectaDNAiRestrictedCleanDeltaCIHigh{}], still in favour of PLECTA in
\PlectaDNAiRestrictedCleanWinCount{} of \PlectaDNAiCleanSceneCount{} scenes.

\section{Depth measures}
\label{sec:app-depth-measures}

The measures of Section~\ref{app:measures} assess which centreline fragments
belong to the same instance and are insensitive to depth: two reconstructions
that agree on every instance but disagree on which strand lies on top at every
crossing receive identical scores. The depth stage is therefore evaluated with
the following measures.

\textbf{Crossing-order accuracy.} Each reference crossing specifies which
instance lies on top; the reconstruction either names one or abstains. Three
quantities are reported, never in isolation: \emph{decided only}, the fraction
of committed decisions that are correct, which alone is not informative
because abstaining on every difficult crossing yields $1.000$; \emph{all
crossings}, which counts an abstention as an error and is the primary
quantity; and the \emph{abstention rate}. The decision is binary and
symmetric, so chance is exactly $\PlectaDepthChance{}$ and only the margin
above it is informative. A predicted crossing is credited only if it involves
the same instance pair as a reference crossing and lies within the placement
tolerance, so spurious crossings cannot improve the score.

\textbf{Depth order over instance pairs.} This global counterpart asks, for a
pair of instances, whether the reconstruction places the deeper one deeper. It
is reported over two sets. Over \emph{crossing pairs}, it measures the stack
where evidence exists. Over \emph{all pairs}, it also includes instances that
never cross, whose relative depth is set by the solver's tie-breaking rule
rather than by the image. The two are never pooled, and the fraction of pairs
that share a connected component is reported alongside them, so that the
second can be interpreted. Both are invariant to monotone rescaling of depth
and therefore describe the arrangement rather than its units. Kendall's
$\tau$ over the same pairs equals $2a-1$ for accuracy $a$ and is not reported
separately.

\textbf{Layer structure.} Layers are an ordinal quotient of depth: a
reconstruction can order every pair correctly but divide the film into a
different number of layers. Layer agreement, the fraction of instances placed
in the reference layer, is therefore reported separately.

\section{Development and evaluation split}
\label{app:split}

All parameters were fixed on a separate development set and never on the
scenes reported in the article. Development used \PlectaDevelopmentSceneCount{}
scenes spanning 20--60\,\% coverage. All segmentation parameters, gates,
penalties and the round schedule were set on these scenes, as was each
comparator's configuration, so that no method was tuned on the scenes on which
it is scored and all were tuned on the same scenes. The evaluation set
comprises \PlectaHeldoutSceneCount{} scenes, 32 at each of 20, 30, 40 and
60\,\% coverage. The generator seeds of both sets are listed in the seed
manifest released with the code.

The synthetic intervals describe sampling variability within this generator;
the ranking is additionally tested on an independently published generator
and on real SEM masks. The generator's shape parameters were also varied.
Changing its bending stiffness from \PlectaCurvinessSweepLow{} to
\PlectaCurvinessSweepHigh{}\,\% of the default, which moves the median
centreline radius from \PlectaCurvinessRadiusHigh{} to
\PlectaCurvinessRadiusLow{}\,px, changed the level means of \Fone{} by at most
\PlectaCurvinessMaxSpread{} over \PlectaCurvinessSeriesCount{} series,
compared with a within-level scene-to-scene standard deviation of
\PlectaCurvinessMinSceneSD{}. Changing the curve wavelength from
\PlectaSmoothLow{} to \PlectaSmoothHigh{}\,px changed them by at most
\PlectaSmoothMaxSpread{}; the one series of \PlectaSmoothSeriesCount{} whose
variation exceeded its own noise is non-monotonic, with its minimum at the
default, and no mechanism for this was identified. Both variations were
measured on development scenes, \PlectaCurvinessSceneCount{} scenes per level,
and are reported as plain means.

\section{Manual annotation procedure}
\label{sec:supp-annotation}

Reference annotations were created with an interactive instance-segmentation
tool for greyscale SEM images of crossing strands, developed in-house. Each
bundle is stored as a separate mask, so that crossing pixels can belong to
several strands, and the saved annotation file is read directly by the
evaluation, without intermediate export or conversion.

Two optional forms of assistance are available during tracing. Strokes can
snap to the local intensity ridge, with polarity calibrated from the existing
annotation, and can be re-rendered as a smoothing spline whose centreline is
shifted to the measured intensity crest only where that crest is consistent.
Apparent widths are measured from perpendicular full-width-at-half-maximum
profiles, referenced to the local minima on either side of the peak; the
reported width is twice the smaller half-width, which limits overestimation
when a crossing strand merges into the profile. These widths are not used by
the centreline-based segmentation scores. The tool proposes gap bridges
between collinear endpoints of different strands, but each proposal must be
accepted by the annotator, so strand identity through crossings and gaps
remains a manual decision.
