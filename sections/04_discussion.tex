\section{Conclusions}
\label{sec:conclusions}
\label{sec:discussion}
\label{sec:limitations}

PLECTA reconstructs overlapping strand instances from a binary strand-axis
mask alone. It solves every junction, of any degree, as a single exact maximum-weight
partial matching with an explicit penalty for unmatched arms, so a strand may
terminate rather than receive a forced continuation, and a crossing pixel is
assigned to every strand that passes through it.

\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=1.5em,label=\textbullet,topsep=6pt,itemsep=2pt,parsep=0pt]
  \item Over \PlectaHeldoutSceneCount{} held-out synthetic scenes from unseen
        seeds it achieved mean pairwise
        \Fone{}=\PlectaHeldoutFOneMean{} [\PlectaHeldoutFOneCILow{},
        \PlectaHeldoutFOneCIHigh{}], falling from
        \PlectaDensityTwoZeroFOneMean{} to \PlectaDensitySixZeroFOneMean{} as
        areal coverage rises from 20 to 60\,\%.
  \item On identical masks it achieved the highest scores against four
        published methods at every density, and the ranking was preserved on
        a competitor's own generator, run unmodified at its published
        settings.
  \item The ablation analysis identified the junction chord-turn term and
        gap bridging as the largest contributors to reconstruction
        performance.
  \item A secondary stage reads the greyscale to order strands in depth. It is
        correct on \PlectaDepthDecidedLow{}--\PlectaDepthDecidedHigh{} of the
        crossings it commits to, against a chance level of
        \PlectaDepthChance{}, and abstains where the image does not separate
        the pair by more than the local noise.
  \item The grouping core uses no random number generator and no training data,
        and its configuration is published, so identical input masks produce
        identical outputs.
  \item The end-to-end workflow does not require manually annotated
        training images: the upstream segmenter is trained on synthetic image
        and mask pairs in which the generating mask provides the label. The
        workflow therefore requires training, but not manual training
        annotations.
\end{itemize}

\noindent\textbf{Scope and limitations.}

\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=1.5em,label=\textbullet,topsep=6pt,itemsep=2pt,parsep=0pt]
  \item Within the generator, performance was insensitive to the evaluated
        strand-shape parameters: sweeping its bending stiffness over
        \PlectaCurvinessSweepLow{}--\PlectaCurvinessSweepHigh{}\,\% of default,
        and its curve wavelength over
        \PlectaSmoothLow{}--\PlectaSmoothHigh{}\,px, moves the level means of
        \Fone{} by at most \PlectaCurvinessMaxSpread{} and, respectively,
        \PlectaSmoothMaxSpread{}, against a scene-to-scene scatter of
        \PlectaCurvinessMinSceneSD{} within a level (\suppsplit{}).
  \item The comparison runs four methods on data none of them was built for. It
        supports a methodological claim: that pairing endpoints greedily, or
        committing with no option to decline, loses identity in dense
        crossings.
  \item Performance remains sensitive to errors in upstream segmentation.
        PLECTA can bridge a short omission, but it cannot infer a strand the mask omits, nor reliably
        reject a false axis introduced upstream.
  \item The real-material demonstration is a case study of
        \PlectaRealFieldCountLower{} CNT SEM fields and cannot estimate
        generalisation. Scored against the annotator whose skeleton it was
        given, PLECTA achieved
        \PlectaInterAnnotatorSameFLow{}--\PlectaInterAnnotatorSameFHigh{}, and
        across annotators
        \PlectaInterAnnotatorCrossFLow{}--\PlectaInterAnnotatorCrossFHigh{},
        while annotators~A and~B agree at
        \PlectaInterAnnotatorAgreeFLow{}--\PlectaInterAnnotatorAgreeFHigh{}
        without algorithmic processing. The disagreement is systematic rather
        than error: B exports
        \PlectaInterAnnotatorObjectRatioLow{}--\PlectaInterAnnotatorObjectRatioHigh{}
        times as many objects as~A, shorter and far more fragmented, which
        reflects systematic differences in the operational definition of a
        strand instance. A real-data score is
        therefore bounded by that disagreement rather than by the method,
        and manual annotation cannot uniquely determine strand identity
        through locally ambiguous occlusions.
  \item Applicability is limited to networks in which directional
        continuity persists across the junction scale; reconstruction
        accuracy may decrease substantially when that assumption is violated,
        as for freely flexible chains.
\end{itemize}

Stronger conclusions would require an independent real-image test set
defined before parameter selection, validated physical-width measurements,
further released implementations evaluated under matched conditions, and an
external-generator benchmark using previously unseen seeds.
