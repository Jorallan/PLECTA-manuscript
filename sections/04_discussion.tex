\section{Discussion and conclusions}
\label{sec:discussion}
\label{sec:limitations}
\label{sec:conclusions}

PLECTA addresses a narrow problem: assigning the fragments of a binary
strand-axis mask to overlapping projected instances, resolving each junction by
mutual compatibility rather than by the cheapest continuation and re-reading
that evidence as chains assemble. Explicit geometry recovers substantial
identity there, reaching \Fone{}=\PlectaHeldoutFOneMean{} over
\PlectaHeldoutSceneCount{} independently seeded held-out scenes. The evidence
is directional, which fixes the condition the image must meet: a tangent
measured on one side of a crossing must predict the arm leaving the other. The
ablations are consistent with that reading, the two largest losses being the
junction chord-turn term and gap matching, both purely geometric. That
is the regime of nanotube bundles and of the cytoskeletal and synthetic-fibre
networks comparable methods target, and not that of freely flexible chains,
where the premise fails rather than degrades. The remaining sensitivity belongs
to the segmenter, since PLECTA can bridge a short omission but cannot infer a
strand the mask omits, nor reliably reject a false axis introduced upstream.

The held-out scenes share the generator family used in development, so their
intervals are sampling variation within it and not transfer; the \emph{ranking}
was checked separately on GraFT's own generator at its published settings and
held there, so what is untested elsewhere is the level and not the comparison.
The comparisons are themselves bounded. The greedy rule is our reimplementation
of a principle rather than of any particular paper, DNAi was run outside the
domain it was built for, and the metric scores how a crossing is
\emph{resolved} and not how it is \emph{represented}. The margin over the
strongest of the four published methods is also the narrowest,
\PlectaRealManualFOneMean{} against \PlectaRealSifneFOneMean{} for SIFNE on the
real fields. What they support is mechanical, that pairing endpoints greedily
or committing with no option to decline loses identity in dense crossings; they
do not establish that PLECTA is generally superior, which would need released
implementations run in their own regimes by their authors.

The real material is weaker ground, deliberately so. The carbon-nanotube
demonstration is a case study of \PlectaRealFieldCountLower{} SEM fields and
cannot estimate generalisation, however closely any two of them agree; its
manual-derived masks are an oracle-like input condition, testing grouping where
the axis evidence is curated rather than automatic SEM-to-instance performance.
Re-annotation by a second reader shows why more readings would not settle it.
Scored against the annotator whose skeleton it was given, PLECTA reaches
\PlectaInterAnnotatorSameFLow{}--\PlectaInterAnnotatorSameFHigh{}, and across
annotators \PlectaInterAnnotatorCrossFLow{}--\PlectaInterAnnotatorCrossFHigh{};
annotators~A and~B agree at
\PlectaInterAnnotatorAgreeFLow{}--\PlectaInterAnnotatorAgreeFHigh{} with no
method in the loop, so the cross-annotator scores sit at roughly the ceiling
human agreement allows, and the automatic-mask condition cannot be separated
from that disagreement. It is systematic: B exports
\PlectaInterAnnotatorObjectRatioLow{}--\PlectaInterAnnotatorObjectRatioHigh{}
times as many objects as A, shorter and far more fragmented: two different
answers to what an instance \emph{is}, not one reader being wrong. Most
directly, given B's skeleton rather than the network's output PLECTA reaches
\PlectaInterAnnotatorBOnAFOneMean{} against A, below what the network's axis
achieves on every field: a second reader is not, on this material, a better
source of an axis than the segmenter is. Identity through an occlusion is what
annotation cannot fix; only in a procedural scene, where identity is assigned
before rendering, is the answer defined rather than adjudicated.

Depth is decided only where crossings supply evidence, so the vertical order it
imposes is scoped to the connected component of the crossing graph, and
ordering a whole film would need evidence that does not travel through
crossings. Rendered width is not validated by a metric that scores ownership of
centreline fragments, so quantitative diameter claims require matched physical
measurements; pairwise fragment scores also emphasise instances with many
fragments, which is why the adjusted Rand index and variation of information
are reported alongside.

The method offers an inspectable route to strand instances that uses no random
number generator and, in its grouping core, no training data; the demonstrated
SEM route is not training-free, its mask coming from a trained CycleGAN and
nnU-Net upstream. Broader claims require independent real fields sealed before
configuration, validated physical widths, further released implementations in a
matched comparison, and an external-generator benchmark with sealed seeds.
