\section{\rev{Materials and methods}}
\label{sec:methods}

\subsection{\rev{Scope and workflow}}
\label{sec:mm-workflow}

\rev{The formal input is a binary filament-axis mask
$M\in\{0,1\}^{H\times W}$. PLECTA reconstructs an overlap-aware collection
$\{I_1,\ldots,I_N\}$, where $I_n\subseteq M$ and a crossing pixel may belong
to several instances. The evaluated grouping stage uses only $M$. A grayscale
SEM image is optional downstream input for width and brightness measurement;
it cannot revise the fixed grouping. Figure~\ref{fig:scope} separates this
core task from upstream mask extraction and downstream rendering.}

\begin{figure}[t]
    \centering
    \includegraphics[width=\linewidth]{figures/fig_plecta_scope.pdf}
    \caption{\rev{Workflow and evidence boundary. A micrograph is converted to
    a binary axis mask manually or by an upstream segmenter. PLECTA groups the
    mask into overlap-aware 2-D instances. SEM characterisation and ribbon
    rendering are optional downstream operations and were absent from the
    held-out grouping result.}}
    \label{fig:scope}
\end{figure}

\subsection{\rev{Synthetic data and evaluation split}}
\label{sec:mm-synth}

\rev{Procedural scenes preserve the ordered centreline $\mathcal C_k$ and
rendered support $\Omega_k$ of every filament $k$. Individual supports remain
separate, so several filaments may own the same crossing pixel. The generator
provides an undegraded one-pixel axis mask, a degraded thin mask containing
small gaps and irregularities, a combined thick foreground, an SEM-like image,
and the exact overlap-aware instance reference (Figure~\ref{fig:synth-examples}).
Areal coverage is the union area of the thick supports divided by image area;
it is not the occupancy of the thin input mask.}

\rev{All evaluated scenes were $512\times512$ px. The recorded generator uses
2-px centreline steps and Gaussian turn increments with standard deviation
0.035 rad, smoothed over 20 px. Path length is sampled uniformly from
0.25--0.85 of the image diagonal; the anchor and initial direction are uniform,
and the longest contiguous in-frame part is retained. Apparent bundle width
and brightness are sampled uniformly from 7--16 px and 0.55--0.98. Filaments
are added until the union of their thick supports reaches the requested
coverage. The degraded axis is obtained by thresholding a longitudinally
modulated thin ridge at 0.27 after correlated edge noise (amplitude 0.06), then
applying recorded hard-cut probability 0.12, crossing-occlusion probability
0.15, a Poisson mean of 2.5 spurious marks, and light morphological
roughening. The primary reconstruction input is the one-pixel version; clean
and 2- and 3-px variants are retained as controlled derivatives of the same
geometry and degradation draw. These are generator settings, not fitted
physical distributions of CNT morphology.}

\rev{Development used 84 scenes: 20 initial scenes across 20--60\% coverage,
48 additional scenes at 20, 30, and 60\%, and 16 scenes at 40\%. The held-out
set comprised 128 different seeds: 32 scenes at each of 20, 30, 40, and 60\%
coverage. It is an independent sample from the same generator, not an
independent distribution. A separate 125-scene locked set remains unevaluated.
The compact seed manifest records every seed block and an executable verifier
checks counts, disjointness, and available scene metadata.}

\rev{A separate controlled robustness set contained 50 scenes, ten at each of
20, 30, 40, 50, and 60\% coverage. Each geometry was scored once from its clean
axis and once from its degraded mask. This set was used only to quantify
input-degradation sensitivity after the configuration was fixed.}

\subsection{\rev{CNT SEM data and mask sources}}
\label{sec:mm-real}

\rev{Two manually annotated CNT-sheet SEM fields were available for exploratory
analysis: B58\_100 ($1024\times1536$ px, 280 filaments) and B58\_110
($1024\times1536$ px, 430 filaments). The nominal horizontal field width is
$1.66~\um$; project metadata associate it with 1536 px, or approximately
$1.08~\mathrm{nm\,px^{-1}}$. The annotation is one human interpretation of
projected, locally ambiguous crossings. Both fields informed development and
neither is a population-level test.}

\rev{Two inputs were derived per field. The first was the skeleton of the manual
instance annotation and is therefore an oracle-input condition, not an
independent segmentation. The second was predicted by an upstream nnU-Net
trained on paired synthetic images and masks; CycleGAN translation is one route
from procedural masks to SEM-like training images
\citep{ruehle2021workflow,allan2026cntsem}. B58\_100 appeared in that model's
training data, whereas B58\_110 was verified absent from the relevant training
set. Consequently, only B58\_110 provides a training-clean observation of mask
transfer, and $n=2$ precludes inference.}

\rev{Mask quality was compared after skeletonisation with a symmetric 3-px
tolerance, avoiding a reward for mask thickness. Reconstruction scores were
computed separately within each mask's visible common-fragment population;
because those populations differ, cross-mask \Fone{} values are descriptive
rather than paired estimates.}

\rev{\textbf{Author metadata required before submission:} SEM instrument and
detector, accelerating voltage, working distance, specimen preparation, CNT
material identity, annotation protocol, and confirmation of the 1536-pixel
calibration against the stored image dimensions. The upstream model archive
must also supply its architecture, training split, and optimisation settings.}

\subsection{\rev{PLECTA reconstruction}}
\label{sec:mm-plecta}

\subsubsection{\rev{Skeleton graph and local frames}}

\rev{PLECTA binarises $M$ at $>0$, skeletonises it, and iteratively removes
spurs no longer than 3 px. Skeleton pixels with degree at least three form
junction clusters, and adjacent clusters are merged into nodes. Connectors up
to 5 px are absorbed into one node; connectors up to 14 px merge nearby nodes
but remain real arms; free debris up to 2 px is absorbed. Each remaining
connected path is stored as an ordered arm with one stub at each end.}

\rev{For ordered points $\mathbf{x}(s)=(r(s),c(s))$ running from a stub tip
into its arm, PLECTA fits each coordinate by least squares in arclength
$0\leq s\leq w$. The fit is quadratic when the observed span is at least
18 px and linear otherwise. With $\det(\mathbf a,\mathbf b)=a_r b_c-a_c b_r$,
the outward frame is}

{\color{revisioncyan}
\begin{equation}
 \mathbf p=\mathbf{x}(0),\qquad
 \mathbf t=-\frac{\mathbf{x}'(0)}{\|\mathbf{x}'(0)\|},\qquad
 \kappa=\frac{\det[-\mathbf{x}'(0),\mathbf{x}''(0)]}
 {\|\mathbf{x}'(0)\|^3}.
 \label{eq:plecta-frame}
\end{equation}}

\rev{A frame is reliable only when at least three samples span at least 3 px.
The initial fit uses $w=24$ px. Subsequent fits may walk through the preceding
round's links and use $w=55$ px, allowing several short arms to provide one
longer directional estimate.}

\rev{For stubs $a,b$, let $d=\|\mathbf p_b-\mathbf p_a\|$ and, when $d>0$,
$\mathbf u=(\mathbf p_b-\mathbf p_a)/d$. The direct reversal, two chord turns,
and chord-confidence factor are}

{\color{revisioncyan}
\begin{equation}
 \theta=\cos^{-1}(\mathbf t_a\!\cdot\!-\mathbf t_b),\quad
 \phi_a=\cos^{-1}(\mathbf t_a\!\cdot\!\mathbf u),\quad
 \phi_b=\cos^{-1}(\mathbf t_b\!\cdot\!-\mathbf u),\quad
 q(d)=\min(1,d/d_0).
 \label{eq:plecta-angles}
\end{equation}
\begin{equation}
 C_{ab}=w_d\theta+w_t q(d)(\phi_a+\phi_b)
       +w_\ell d/\ell_0+30w_\kappa|\kappa_a+\kappa_b|.
 \label{eq:plecta-cost}
\end{equation}}

\rev{For coincident tips the two chord angles are set to zero. Junction costs
set $w_\ell=0$; gap costs retain the length term. Thus the tangent-reversal
term remains usable at a rasterised crossing, while $q(d)$ suppresses the
poorly determined chord direction until its length reaches $d_0$.}

\subsubsection{\rev{Candidate gates and exact matching}}

\rev{Let $S$ be the stubs considered at one junction, or all free stubs in the
gap stage; let $E$ contain the geometrically admissible pairs; and let $p_i$ be
the price for leaving stub $i$ unmatched. PLECTA solves the exact partial
matching problem}

{\color{revisioncyan}
\begin{equation}
 M^*=\underset{M\subseteq E\,:\,M\ \mathrm{is\ a\ matching}}{\arg\min}
 \left[\sum_{(i,j)\in M}C_{ij}
 +\sum_{i\in S\setminus V(M)}p_i\right].
 \label{eq:plecta-matching}
\end{equation}}

\rev{It is implemented as unconstrained-cardinality maximum-weight matching
with edge weight $p_i+p_j-C_{ij}$; edges with $C_{ij}\geq p_i+p_j$ are omitted.
Every junction is solved independently, with candidates restricted to
different arms sharing that node. At full strictness $p_i=0.62$, so a junction
edge requires $C_{ij}<1.24$. The configured gap-relief term is zero, hence all
junction stubs have the same unmatched price.}

\rev{Gap candidates are constructed only from still-unmatched, reliable stubs.
They must belong to different arms and different junctions, lie within 85 px,
have direct tangent mismatch at most 0.40 rad, and have chord-turn mismatch at
each end at most 0.28 rad. Global exact matching then accepts only edges whose
cost is below 0.60 at full strictness.}

\rev{For rounds $r=0,\ldots,7$, the strictness factor is
$\alpha_r=0.85+0.15r/7$. Only the junction and gap unmatched prices and the two
gap-angle limits are multiplied by $\alpha_r$; distance and all cost weights
remain fixed. Each round estimates frames from the preceding matching, remakes
all junction links, removes cycles, remakes all gap links, and removes cycles
again. In the arm-link graph, a component with $n$ arms closes a cycle when it
contains at least $n$ accepted links; PLECTA repeatedly deletes that component's
matched pair with the greatest tip-to-tip distance until it is open. No cycle
was encountered in the 84 development scenes. Only $r=7$ has
$\alpha_r=1$, so its full-strictness matching is returned; the objective does
not select an earlier, deliberately under-linked annealing round.}

\subsubsection{\rev{Instance output}}

\rev{Connected components of accepted arm links define instances. PLECTA
paints every arm pixel and every junction cluster touched by a chain into that
instance, so intersecting instances share crossing pixels. An isolated one-arm
component shorter than 6 px is omitted. Accepted gap links determine identity
but are not painted into the fixed centreline output. All effective parameters,
including values previously inherited as code defaults, are materialised in the
fixed JSON configuration.}

\subsection{\rev{Optional SEM characterisation and ribbon rendering}}
\label{sec:mm-stage4}

\rev{After grouping, perpendicular SEM profiles are sampled every 2 px along
each ordered chain and at least 6 px from a junction. Profiles are recentred by
at most 3 px and accepted only when contrast exceeds both four background-noise
standard deviations and 0.05 normalised intensity; fitted widths must lie
between 1.5 and 36 px. At least three valid cuts are required. These
measurements attach apparent width, brightness, uncertainty, and a quality flag
to an existing instance; they cannot alter its arm membership.}

\rev{Optional ribbon rendering interpolates accepted gaps, resamples the
centreline at 1 px, smooths it over 9 px with fixed endpoints, and draws the
chain at its fitted width clipped to 3--40 px. It excludes junction blobs and
does not clip the ribbon back to the input mask. Silhouette absorption would
union rendered instances, but its threshold is zero by default. No absorption
or later post-hoc continuation merge contributed to the 128-scene held-out
result; neither is presented as part of the evaluated algorithm.}

\rev{Synthetic width recovery was assessed only on the 84-scene development
set. A measured chain was associated with a rendered reference filament when
at least 90\% of its sampled centreline belonged to that filament. Apparent
width was then compared with the generator value for the associated filament;
interval calibration used scene-level cross-validation so that cuts from one
scene could not occur in both calibration and assessment folds. This validates
measurement of the synthetic renderer, not physical CNT diameter.}

\subsection{\rev{Metrics and statistical analysis}}
\label{sec:mm-metrics}

\rev{The primary endpoint is common-fragment pairwise \Fone{}. For each input
mask, the scorer skeletonises once, iteratively prunes junction-touching
free-end spurs of at most 3 px, removes pixels with at least three
8-neighbours, labels the remaining 8-connected pieces, and discards pieces
shorter than 6 px. This partition is independent of PLECTA and of the reference
instances.}

\rev{Each retained fragment is assigned separately to the overlap-aware
reference layers and prediction layers. The instance with the largest direct
pixel overlap is selected if it covers at least 30\% of the fragment. Otherwise,
instance masks are flattened deterministically in decreasing area and then
increasing identifier order; the fragment is assigned to the most frequent
nearest flattened label among its pixels lying within 6 px of an instance.
Ties resolve to the smaller identifier. If neither rule finds a candidate, the
fragment is unassigned.}

\rev{Let $n_{ij}$ be the number of fragments assigned to reference group $i$
and predicted group $j$, with $a_i=\sum_jn_{ij}$ and $b_j=\sum_i n_{ij}$.
Every unassigned fragment is treated as its own singleton rather than placing
all missing evidence in one artificial group. The pair counts are}

{\color{revisioncyan}
\begin{equation}
 \mathrm{TP}=\sum_{ij}\binom{n_{ij}}{2},\qquad
 \mathrm{FP}=\sum_j\binom{b_j}{2}-\mathrm{TP},\qquad
 \mathrm{FN}=\sum_i\binom{a_i}{2}-\mathrm{TP}.
 \label{eq:pair-counts}
\end{equation}}

\rev{A true-positive pair therefore shares both identities; a false-positive
pair joins different reference filaments; and a false-negative pair separates
fragments of one reference filament. Thus}

\begin{equation}
    \rev{P=\frac{\mathrm{TP}}{\mathrm{TP}+\mathrm{FP}},\qquad
    R=\frac{\mathrm{TP}}{\mathrm{TP}+\mathrm{FN}},\qquad
    \Fone{}=\frac{2PR}{P+R}.}
    \label{eq:pairwise-f1}
\end{equation}

\rev{This is a clustering metric related to the Rand family
\citep{rand1971objective,argandacarreras2015crowdsourcing}. It scores grouping,
not rendered width, and weights filaments with many fragments more strongly
because their pair count is quadratic. We therefore also report adjusted Rand
index \citep{hubert1985comparing}, variation of information split and merge
components \citep{meila2007comparing}, and the fraction of reference filaments
recovered by one dominant predicted instance using 0.8 purity and coverage
thresholds.}

\rev{Scores are computed per scene and reported summaries are arithmetic means
over successfully scored scenes, rather than one pooled contingency table.
A scene with fewer than two fragments, or with no positive reference or
predicted pairs, has no pair evidence and its undefined precision or recall is
retained as missing rather than replaced by a favourable value. The evaluation
harness writes an explicit error row for any failed scene; such rows are counted
as failures and excluded from metric means. The reported 128-scene held-out run
had no failures.}

\rev{A diagnostic connectivity floor assigns one label to each 8-connected
component of the same held-out input mask and is scored without tuning by the
same common-fragment procedure. It tests whether the benchmark can be solved by
connectivity alone; it is not treated as a competitive filament tracer.}

\rev{Held-out means and density strata are accompanied by 95\% nonparametric
bootstrap intervals (20,000 replicates; seed 20,260,810). Aggregate resampling
is stratified by density. The clean-minus-degraded analysis resamples paired
scene differences within density. Active-component ablations are one-at-a-time
development experiments on all 84 development scenes and are labelled as such;
they do not reuse the held-out set.}
