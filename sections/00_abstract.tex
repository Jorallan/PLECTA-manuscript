Converting a dense filament centreline mask into individual bundle instances
is difficult: gaps break true trajectories, and crossings create several
locally plausible continuations. We present FilaSeg, a deterministic
geometry-based method that takes a binary carbon-nanotube (CNT) bundle-axis
mask and separates it into individual bundle instances through staged,
topology-checked geometric reconnection; a final stage then uses the paired
scanning electron microscopy (SEM) image to estimate each instance's apparent
width and render it. Methods are compared on a common, method-independent
partition of the input skeleton into fragments, using pairwise grouping
\Fone{} on equivalent per-instance centreline representations, evaluated
before width rendering. After the evaluation configuration was frozen,
FilaSeg and a minimum-turn skeleton tracer were evaluated on
\LockedSceneCount{} independently seeded procedural scenes. FilaSeg achieved
mean common-fragment \Fone{}=\LockedFilaSegCommonFOneMean{}
(density-stratified 95\% bootstrap CI [\LockedFilaSegCommonFOneCILow{},
\LockedFilaSegCommonFOneCIHigh{}]), with a paired
FilaSeg-minus-minimum-turn difference of
\FilaSegMinusBaselineSkeletonCommonFOneMean{} (95\% CI
[\FilaSegMinusBaselineSkeletonCommonFOneCILow{},
\FilaSegMinusBaselineSkeletonCommonFOneCIHigh{}]). This advantage was not
uniform: it held at four of the five tested densities, and its interval
crossed zero at the fifth. Scoring the rendered Stage-4 output instead of the
Stage-3 centreline gave a lower score, showing that grouping accuracy and
rendering accuracy must be assessed separately. A single manually annotated
SEM crop was used during development and is not a held-out test. Taken
together, this predefined synthetic evaluation supports a conditional
advantage over the fixed minimum-turn comparator for the tested generator; it
does not establish general superiority over filament-tracing methods, or
transfer to other SEM instruments, materials, or upstream segmenters.
