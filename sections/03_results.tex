\subsection{Published methods on identical masks}
\label{sec:res-comparators}

This section compares PLECTA against methods published by others. Our own
controls, which isolate parts of the formulation rather than compare against
anyone, are reported separately in Section~\ref{sec:res-ablation}.

Four published methods are scored beside PLECTA on identical masks, through
the same conversion and metric code, over \PlectaGreedySceneCount{} scenes
generated after every configuration was fixed. DNAi \citep{playout2026dnai},
GraFT \citep{osterlund2026graft} and SIFNE \citep{zhang2017sifne} are run from
their own released sources; SIFNE is marked with a dagger because one shipped
setting is degenerate at these densities and was re-selected on development
scenes, as DNAi's was. The fourth, \citet{basu2015extraction}, never released
an implementation and is reimplemented here; it is marked with a star wherever
it appears, and what each mark costs the comparison is stated below.

Every comparator was gated on parity before any of its numbers was read: none
was believed until the conversion reproduced PLECTA's stored \Fone{}, adjusted Rand index
and both variation-of-information components, because a faulty conversion
yields plausible rather than obviously wrong scores. Each comparator's
parameter translation was selected on the same \PlectaDevelopmentSceneCount{}
development scenes PLECTA was configured on, and every adaptation was checked
and found to favour the comparator; \suppcomparators{} records each
one and how each method fails.

defaults it scores \Fone{}=\PlectaDNAiShippedFOne{} on these masks against
\PlectaDNAiFOne{} tuned. Tuned, the paired difference is \PlectaDNAiDeltaFOne{}
[\PlectaDNAiDeltaCILow{}, \PlectaDNAiDeltaCIHigh{}] over
\PlectaDNAiSceneCount{} scenes, and \PlectaDNAiCleanDeltaFOne{}
[\PlectaDNAiCleanDeltaCILow{}, \PlectaDNAiCleanDeltaCIHigh{}] on clean axes,
the gap widening monotonically with density
(Figure~\ref{fig:metric-panels}).

DNAi's margin is a limitation of domain and density, not a general ranking: it
is built for DNA-fibre micrographs, which are sparse, and where the network is
sparse and the axis clean it recovers \PlectaDNAiCleanSparseFOne{} at 20\%
coverage. Restricting the score to the evidence a degree-limited, gap-blind
method can reach separates capability from judgement, and on clean axes the
difference falls from \PlectaDNAiCleanDeltaFOne{} to
\PlectaDNAiRestrictedCleanDeltaFOne{} [\PlectaDNAiRestrictedCleanDeltaCILow{},
\PlectaDNAiRestrictedCleanDeltaCIHigh{}], still favouring PLECTA in
\PlectaDNAiRestrictedCleanWinCount{} of \PlectaDNAiCleanSceneCount{} scenes and
at every coverage level. Roughly \PlectaDNAiCoverageShareClean{}\,\% of the
margin is therefore coverage of cases the comparator cannot attempt, and
\PlectaDNAiDecisionShareClean{}\,\% the outcome of decisions both methods make.

\textbf{GraFT.} Its published entry point begins with a Frangi and Otsu front
end for greyscale ridge images, which has nothing to do on a two-valued axis,
so the mask was injected at its skeleton-to-graph stage, everything downstream
being its own code unchanged. That substitution favours it: run end to end on
the greyscale images it scores \PlectaGraFTNativeFOne{}, because its
segmentation stage recovers only a third to a half of the reference skeleton.
Its minimum continuation angle was also re-tuned on development scenes. GraFT
scored \PlectaGraFTFOne{}, a paired difference of \PlectaGraFTDeltaFOne{}
[\PlectaGraFTDeltaCILow{}, \PlectaGraFTDeltaCIHigh{}] favouring PLECTA in every
one of the \PlectaGraFTSceneCount{} scenes both methods completed, and
\PlectaGraFTCleanDeltaFOne{} [\PlectaGraFTCleanDeltaCILow{},
\PlectaGraFTCleanDeltaCIHigh{}] on clean axes. \PlectaGraFTFailed{} of the
densest degraded scenes did not finish inside a 45-minute budget and are
excluded from its means; they are the hardest scenes of the set, so that
exclusion flatters GraFT. Neither released method establishes a general
ranking, and Section~\ref{sec:limitations} states what these comparisons leave
untested.

\textbf{SIFNE.} It is the released method whose junction handling is closest to
the greedy rule this paper uses as a floor: it excises each crossing, estimates
tip directions from the stubs left behind, and pairs termini inside an angular
fan. Everything after its thinning step reads a binary skeleton, so the mask is
injected where its own pipeline re-enters and every stage that assigns identity
is its code. One shipped setting is degenerate here and was re-selected on
development scenes, as DNAi's linkage distance was: its erasure neighbourhood
is applied at every crossing pixel, and because the erasures multiply with the
crossings it discards between a third and two thirds of the input mask before
any pairing is attempted. That change is worth \PlectaSifneShippedFOne{} to
\PlectaSifneFOne{} \Fone{}, and both are reported. Tuned, it is the strongest
comparator here, ahead of GraFT and of the greedy rule, and its Crossing
Fidelity of \PlectaSifneCrossingFidelity{} is second only to PLECTA's. The
margin against it therefore rests on a method configured for this data rather
than on a default that suits it badly, which is the comparison worth making.
On a rebuilt version of the cobweb topology its own paper validates against,
the two are level where that paper works, \PlectaSifneOwnSparsePlectaFOne{}
against \PlectaSifneOwnSparseSifneFOne{} at
\PlectaSifneOwnSparseDensity{} centreline pixels per image pixel, and separate
only slightly at \PlectaSifneOwnDenseDensity{},
\PlectaSifneOwnDensePlectaFOne{} against \PlectaSifneOwnDenseSifneFOne{}; that
its deficit here is far larger at the same density on tangled scenes places the
difference in the crossing geometry rather than in the density.
\suppcomparators{} gives the injection point, the selection, the reconstruction
and what was not attempted.

\textbf{Curvature-priced recombination, reimplemented.} The one prior method
that both optimises over a neighbourhood and prices its joins on curvature is
that of \citet{basu2015extraction}, so it is the closest structural relative
PLECTA's junction matching has. Its software was never released, so it is
reimplemented here and reported as a floor, exactly as the greedy rule is,
never as an externally run result. Only its Section~II-B is implemented: a
spanning tree over the mask points, every bifurcation deleted, and the
surviving chains recombined by a set-partitioning integer program that prices
each candidate by the curvature energy of a fitted spline less a fixed bond
energy per join. Its intensity-guided front end is absent, so the skeleton
stands in for the particle cloud it would produce, and its two parameters were
selected on the same development scenes as every other comparator's.

It reaches \Fone{}=\PlectaBasuFOne{}, above GraFT and below the greedy rule,
and it over-segments: \PlectaBasuInstanceRatio{} times as many instances as the
reference. The informative result is the split between its two scores. On
Crossing Fidelity it reaches \PlectaBasuCrossingFidelity{}, second only to
PLECTA and ahead of both released methods, while its pairwise \Fone{} sits far
below theirs. Crossing Fidelity counts junction decisions and is not quadratic
in fragmentation; the pairwise score is both. So the curvature-priced matching
is doing real work at the junctions themselves, and what costs it is
everything around that decision: deleting every bifurcation shatters a scene
faster than a recombination limited to three segments per round can rebuild
it, and the formulation carries no gap model at all. That is the distinction
this comparison exists to draw, and it is why the priced refusal in
Section~\ref{sec:mm-plecta} is claimed as part of a combination rather than on
its own.

\textbf{A second generator.} Every scene above comes from one generator, so the
comparison was repeated on GraFT's own, run unmodified at its published settings
over \PlectaRegimeSceneCount{} scenes in \PlectaRegimeStratumCount{} density
strata spanning \PlectaRegimeDensityLow{} to \PlectaRegimeDensityHigh{}
centreline pixels per image pixel; GraFT's published comparison reaches
\PlectaRegimeDensityPublished{}, so its whole range is the lower half of this
one. PLECTA leads at every density, from \PlectaRegimeLowPlectaFOne{} against
\PlectaRegimeLowGraFTFOne{} and \PlectaRegimeLowDNAiFOne{} at the sparsest to
\PlectaRegimeHighPlectaFOne{} against \PlectaRegimeHighGraFTFOne{} and
\PlectaRegimeHighDNAiFOne{} at the densest; the margin widens with density and
nothing crosses over, including inside GraFT's published range. The
field-standard measures order the three identically over the whole ladder
(adjusted Rand index \PlectaRegimeOverallPlectaARI{},
\PlectaRegimeOverallGraFTARI{} and \PlectaRegimeOverallDNAiARI{}; variation of
information \PlectaRegimeOverallPlectaVITotal{},
\PlectaRegimeOverallGraFTVITotal{} and \PlectaRegimeOverallDNAiVITotal{} bits),
and PLECTA's errors lean mildly to fusion where both comparators divide far more
than they fuse. GraFT was also run end to end with its own front end, which on
this data is inside its design envelope rather than degenerate as it is on ours;
that arm scored \PlectaRegimeLowGraFTNativeFOne{} and
\PlectaRegimeHighGraFTNativeFOne{} at the two ends, below the injected arm
almost everywhere, so injecting the mask continues to favour it. The held-out
result of Section~\ref{sec:res-density} is a same-generator measurement and
remains one; this comparison is not.

\textbf{Crossings resolved.} The Crossing Fidelity row of
Table~\ref{tab:plecta-comparators} states the contribution directly, and pooled over the
\PlectaJunctionCount{} reference crossings of the degraded set PLECTA
partitions \PlectaJunctionPlectaRate{} of them exactly against
\PlectaJunctionGreedyRate{} for the greedy baseline,
\PlectaJunctionGraFTRate{} for GraFT and \PlectaJunctionDNAiRate{} for DNAi;
paired, PLECTA's margins are \PlectaJunctionGreedyDelta{}
[\PlectaJunctionGreedyDeltaCILow{}, \PlectaJunctionGreedyDeltaCIHigh{}],
\PlectaJunctionGraFTDelta{} [\PlectaJunctionGraFTDeltaCILow{},
\PlectaJunctionGraFTDeltaCIHigh{}] and \PlectaJunctionDNAiDelta{}
[\PlectaJunctionDNAiDeltaCILow{}, \PlectaJunctionDNAiDeltaCIHigh{}]. Clean axes
order them the same way, PLECTA at \PlectaJunctionCleanPlectaRate{}. Leaving
every arm unpaired resolves \PlectaJunctionChance{} of these crossings, which is
the all-unpaired baseline the rate is read against; it is a deterministic
do-nothing policy rather than a chance level. It is much less discriminative than
the pairwise score, spanning \PlectaJunctionSpreadHigh{} between best and worst
method at 60\,\% coverage where pairwise \Fone{} spans
\PlectaPairwiseSpreadHigh{}, so it is used to say what was solved and not to
rank. Everyone is poor at the busiest nodes: above degree four PLECTA resolves
\PlectaJunctionPlectaDegreeFivePlus{} and no comparator does better, while DNAi
collapses to \PlectaJunctionDNAiDegreeFivePlus{}, which is what its degree-four
cap predicts.

Per-scene cost was measured warm, single-threaded and pinned to one core, timing
each method's own computation only (Table~\ref{tab:plecta-comparators};
\RuntimeGraftCovSixtyCensored{} GraFT scenes exceeded \RuntimeBudgetSeconds{}~s
and are censored). These are independently optimised codebases, so absolute
times measure implementation as much as algorithm; the transferable quantity is
scaling, and the log--log exponents against foreground pixels are
\RuntimeDnaiScalingExponent{}, \RuntimePlectaScalingExponent{} and at least
\RuntimeGraftScalingExponent{}. PLECTA has the largest uncensored exponent;
GraFT's is a lower bound, because the scenes its fit lost to censoring are the
slowest ones, and only GraFT is heavy-tailed.

\subsection{Crossing representation, and where it over-marks}
\label{sec:res-overlap}

Resolving a crossing and representing one are different things, and only the
first is what the scores above measure. Which arm continues into which is the
difficult decision, and every score in this paper rewards getting it right.
Whether the emitted raster \emph{represents} a crossing pixel as belonging to
both strands is a property of the output format, and the metric cannot see
it: flattening the emitted layers to a single label image leaves PLECTA's
\Fone{} unchanged to four decimal places (\PlectaOverlapFlattenCost{}).

The representation is instead verifiable directly: on the comparison set,
reference strands share \PlectaOverlapReferenceLow{}\,\% of their pixels with
another strand at 20\% coverage, rising to
\PlectaOverlapReferenceHigh{}\,\% at 60\%; PLECTA emits
\PlectaOverlapExpressedLow{}--\PlectaOverlapExpressedHigh{}\,\%, and GraFT,
whose output is close to a hard partition, about
\PlectaOverlapGraFTHigh{}\,\%. Which pixels those are is less flattering.
PLECTA recovers \PlectaSharedRecall{} of the pixels the reference gives to more
than one strand, less than half, though the largest such recall here, against
\PlectaSharedGraFTRecall{} for GraFT and \PlectaSharedDNAiRecall{} for DNAi,
while the greedy baseline, a hard partition, scores zero by construction. It
does so at a precision of \PlectaSharedPrecision{}, marking
\PlectaSharedExcess{}--\PlectaSharedCleanExcess{} times more shared pixels than
the reference contains, because a chain is painted with every pixel of every
junction cluster it touches. That is a property of the rendering rather than of
the grouping, and one the \Fone{} neither rewards nor penalises.

Figure~\ref{fig:examples} shows what the score corresponds to scene by scene,
taking at three coverage levels the scene whose \Fone{} is closest to the
median, so the rows are typical rather than selected.

\subsection{Response to the input mask}
\label{sec:res-mask}

On the separate \PlectaRobustnessSceneCount{}-scene controlled set,
mean \Fone{} was \PlectaRobustnessDegradedFOneMean{} from degraded axes and
\PlectaRobustnessCleanFOneMean{} from the corresponding clean axes. The paired
clean-minus-degraded change was \PlectaCleanMinusDegradedFOneMean{} (95\% CI
[\PlectaCleanMinusDegradedFOneCILow{},
\PlectaCleanMinusDegradedFOneCIHigh{}]). Modelled gaps and raster
irregularities therefore caused a small resolved loss under this generator.

The thickness of the axis mask costs more, and it matters because it is the
upstream segmenter, not the method, that fixes it: over
\PlectaSensitivityWidthSceneCount{} development scenes a nominal
\PlectaSensitivityWidthHigh{}\,px axis mask scored
\PlectaSensitivityWidthDeltaFOne{} \Fone{}
[\PlectaSensitivityWidthDeltaCILow{}, \PlectaSensitivityWidthDeltaCIHigh{}]
against the \PlectaSensitivityWidthLow{}\,px mask of the same scenes. Each width
variant is scored against common fragments rebuilt from its own input mask, so
the question asked is how well the method groups the skeleton it is handed, and
the decline with width is a thicker mask skeletonising into a messier graph
rather than a change in the grouping problem. This is the one density axis that
does move the score. The rendered silhouette does not, causally and scene by
scene (Section~\ref{sec:res-factorial}), so a mask should be reported by the
geometry of its axis and not by how much of the frame its bundles fill.

\subsection{Development ablations and downstream rendering}
\label{sec:res-ablation}

\textbf{Two controls isolate the formulation.} Neither is anyone else's
method, which is exactly what makes them useful: each holds the evidence
fixed and varies one thing, so the margin measures the formulation rather
than two whole systems. They are reported here rather than in
Section~\ref{sec:res-comparators}, whose subject is published work.

\textbf{Greedy continuation.} The baseline skeletonises the same mask and
greedily accepts the lowest-turning-angle pairing between branch ends inside
distance and turning-angle gates fixed by a development sweep. It is ours --
not a reimplementation of any paper but of the pairing principle shared by
SIFNE, GraFT's path cover and Wegmayr et
al.\ \citep{zhang2017sifne,osterlund2026graft,wegmayr2020microplastic} -- and
is reported as a transparent floor, never as an external result. PLECTA leads
it by \PlectaGreedySweptDeltaFOne{} \Fone{}
[\PlectaGreedySweptDeltaCILow{}, \PlectaGreedySweptDeltaCIHigh{}] paired over
the \PlectaGreedySceneCount{} scenes, and by
\PlectaGreedySweptCleanDeltaFOne{} [\PlectaGreedySweptCleanDeltaCILow{},
\PlectaGreedySweptCleanDeltaCIHigh{}] on clean axes, so the margin is not an
artefact of mask degradation.

\textbf{Exact against greedy matching, matcher only.} That baseline replaces
the whole linker; a second arm isolates the claim, keeping the graph, cost,
admissibility gate and round schedule and replacing only the per-junction
maximum-weight matching by greedy cheapest-first acceptance. Exactness was
worth \PlectaMatcherDeltaFOne{} \Fone{} [\PlectaMatcherDeltaCILow{},
\PlectaMatcherDeltaCIHigh{}] over \PlectaMatcherSceneCount{} development
scenes, on \PlectaMatcherWinCount{} of \PlectaMatcherSceneCount{} of them, and
\PlectaMatcherCleanDeltaFOne{} [\PlectaMatcherCleanDeltaCILow{},
\PlectaMatcherCleanDeltaCIHigh{}] on clean axes; the failure is division, not
merging, with variation-of-information split rising
\PlectaMatcherExactVISplit{} to \PlectaMatcherGreedyVISplit{} at unchanged
merge, \PlectaMatcherInstanceExcess{}\,\% more strands emitted and crossings
resolved exactly falling \PlectaMatcherExactJunctionRate{} to
\PlectaMatcherGreedyJunctionRate{}. The relative loss grows monotonically with
degree, \PlectaMatcherDegreeThreeRelative{}\,\% at degree three to
\PlectaMatcherDegreeSixRelative{}\,\% at degree six, but in absolute terms
peaks at degree four (\PlectaMatcherDegreeFourAbsolute{}), where most crossings
lie; and the greedy arm runs at parameters selected with the exact matcher in
the loop, a bias in that matcher's favour.

One-at-a-time ablations were evaluated only on the
\PlectaDevelopmentSceneCount{} development scenes
(Table~\ref{tab:plecta-ablation}).
The three largest losses relative to the evaluated configuration were, in
order, the junction chord-turn term
(\PlectaAblationTangentTermOnlyDeltaFOne{}), gap matching
(\PlectaAblationNoGapBridgingDeltaFOne{}) and connector-based junction-cluster
consolidation (\PlectaAblationNoCrossingMergingAtAllDeltaFOne{}); every other
component, chain-extended frames and the round schedule included, mattered
less. These are development values, not held-out effect estimates.

The core output is a grouping, not a set of connected curves: accepted gap
links establish identity but are not painted, so on the development set
\PlectaCoreDisconnected{} of core strands arrive as more than one disjoint
pixel run and \PlectaCoreBranched{} contain a branch point. Any downstream step
that assumes one connected curve per strand must account for this. The
optional ribbon rendering of Section~\ref{sec:mm-stage4}, at its defaults,
scored development \Fone{}=\PlectaStageFourFOne{}, a change of
\PlectaStageFourDeltaFOne{}, and reduced those fractions to
\PlectaStageFourDisconnected{} and \PlectaStageFourBranched{}, so rendering buys
connectivity at a small cost in the identity score without changing chain
membership. Enabling the separate 0.6 silhouette-absorption rule reduced
\Fone{} further to \PlectaStageFourAbsorbFOne{}, and because absorption can
merge identities it remained disabled. None of these topology measurements
validates apparent width.

\subsection{Proof of concept on real SEM}
\label{sec:res-real}
\label{sec:res-qualitative}

The claim of this section is one sentence: PLECTA works on real imaging when a
second technique supplies the mask, and Pipeline~A is such a technique. Nothing
here is a measurement of Pipeline~A, which carries its own evaluation
\citep{allan2026cntsem}.

Pipeline~A runs procedural mask $\rightarrow$ CycleGAN $\rightarrow$ paired
synthetic corpus $\rightarrow$ nnU-Net $\rightarrow$ binary strand-axis mask:
a generator draws a mask, a CycleGAN translates it into an SEM-like image so
that image and mask are paired without an annotator, an nnU-Net trained on
those pairs segments a real micrograph, and its output is a binary axis mask
\citep{ruehle2021workflow,allan2026cntsem}. Pipeline~B is PLECTA, and it is the
only stage evaluated here. \PlectaRealFieldCountWord{} manually annotated
CNT-sheet fields separate the two: PLECTA is run once on the skeleton of
annotator~A's annotation and once on the nnU-Net axis, with annotator~A's
annotation as the reference in both cases, so the two conditions differ only
in the mask. The first is an oracle-like input condition rather than an
end-to-end result, asking what the grouping can do when the axis evidence is
curated; only the second says what an automatic mask allows.
Figures~\ref{fig:real-sem}
and~\ref{fig:real-sem-axis} show both on one field, at width and as the
centrelines the scores are computed on.

\subsection{Optional depth decomposition}
\label{sec:res-depth}

The stages above reconstruct strands in projection. A crossing pixel is
assigned to both strands that own it, but nothing so far says which of the
two lies on top. The optional depth stage answers that from the greyscale
alone -- the continuity of each strand's own brightness across the crossing,
weighed against the noise measured there -- then imposes one
globally consistent vertical order and places each strand at a height at
which nothing interpenetrates. It changes no 2-D strand.

reference 2-D geometry, the depth decision is measured alone; given only the

The decomposition is the result. Order accuracy over decided crossings spans
\PlectaDepthDecidedSpan{}
(\PlectaDepthDecidedLow{}--\PlectaDepthDecidedHigh{}) across both conditions
and both densities, while accuracy over all crossings spans more than three
times that, \PlectaDepthAllSpan{}
(\PlectaDepthAllLow{}--\PlectaDepthAllHigh{}) -- and it
falls by the amount the crossing recovery rate falls,
\PlectaDepthMatchHigh{} to \PlectaDepthMatchLow{}: at the denser coverage the
reconstructed condition's \PlectaDepthCleanCovFourZeroAll{} over all crossings,
divided by the given condition's \PlectaDepthOracleCovFourZeroAll{}, reproduces
its crossing recovery rate \PlectaDepthCleanCovFourZeroMatch{} to within two
thousandths. The decided figures are only meaningful beside the abstention row
above them: what the stage declines there are the hard crossings, so a higher
decided accuracy is a stage answering less often, not a stage deciding better.
Once a crossing is found, the decision is made at a broadly similar accuracy
whether the geometry was given or reconstructed, and whether the scene carries
the sparser crossing load or roughly five times it, though not at the same
accuracy: the widest gap between the input conditions is
\PlectaDepthCleanCovTwoZeroDecided{} reconstructed against
\PlectaDepthOracleCovTwoZeroDecided{} given, at the sparser coverage and in
favour of the reconstruction, which scores only the crossings it matched and
abstains on more of them. What limits
the depth result is the 2-D stage not recovering every crossing, not the
ordering of the crossings it does recover.

The global stack is weaker than the local decision, and the gap is a property
of the evidence rather than of the decision. Depth order is recovered at
\PlectaDepthCleanCovTwoZeroCrossOrder{} over pairs that cross but only
\PlectaDepthCleanCovTwoZeroAllOrder{} over all pairs, because depth propagates
solely along crossings: two strands in different components of the crossing
graph are related by no evidence whatever, and their order is a stated
tie-break. That is measurable, not merely arguable. Only
\PlectaDepthWithinLow{} to \PlectaDepthWithinHigh{} of strand pairs lie
within one component, and if the pairs that do are ordered at the
crossing-pair rate while the rest are a coin flip, the all-pairs figure follows
arithmetically: at 20\,\% coverage that predicts
\PlectaDepthCleanCovTwoZeroOrderPred{} against
\PlectaDepthCleanCovTwoZeroAllOrder{} measured. The arithmetic is an
approximation in both directions rather than an account that holds at one end
and fails at the other: it under-predicts at the sparser coverage, where the
pairs in different components are evidently not quite a coin flip, and
over-predicts at the denser end, where the measured order falls below the
prediction by up to
\PlectaDepthOrderResidual{}, consistent with error accumulating along the long
transitive chains that large components create. Depth claims are therefore
scoped to the connected component, and a globally ordered film would need
evidence that does not travel through crossings, such as defocus, or a second view, rather than a smoothness assumption, which these scenes would not support:
their heights are drawn independently, so proximity carries no depth
information by construction.

Layer agreement follows the same pattern, falling from
\PlectaDepthCleanCovTwoZeroLayers{} to \PlectaDepthCleanCovFourZeroLayers{} as
coverage doubles while the recovered layer count stays close to the reference:
the ordering is locally right, so the number of layers is about right, while
small order errors move strands across layer boundaries.
