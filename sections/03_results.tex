\subsection{Representation audit}
\label{sec:res-representation}

The post hoc evaluator audit changed neither method outputs nor the locked
configuration. It reproduced the stored legacy Stage-4 \Fone{} values exactly,
then rescored equivalent representations with ground-truth-unassigned common
fragments retained as singletons. Reducing the Stage-4 output to centrelines
gave mean \Fone{}=\LockedFilaSegStageFourCenterlineCommonFOneMean{}, whereas the
rendered Stage-4 masks gave
\LockedFilaSegStageFourRenderedCommonFOneMean{}. Relative to the Stage-3
centreline endpoint, the paired changes were
\FilaSegStageFourCenterlineMinusStageThreeCommonFOneMean{} and
\FilaSegStageFourRenderedMinusStageThreeCommonFOneMean{}, respectively. These
are two negative representation diagnostics, not an exact mechanistic
decomposition: skeletonizing rendered instance layers can shift their
centrelines. The rendered output is scientifically relevant to downstream use,
but it is not a representation-neutral grouping endpoint.

\subsection{Development sensitivity studies}
\label{sec:res-mask}
\label{sec:res-width}
\label{sec:res-clean}

Input-mask width was assessed on the 20-scene development set, not on the locked
benchmark. The one-pixel mask had the highest mean common \Fone{}. The paired
interval for the two-pixel mask included zero, whereas the three-pixel mask
showed a resolved decrease. Thus the tested thin representation was preferable,
but the two-pixel comparison did not resolve a difference at the 95\% level.

\begin{figure}[t]
  \centering
  \includegraphics[width=0.82\linewidth]{figures/fig_width_sweep.pdf}
  \caption{Development-only sensitivity to axis-mask width. Thin lines connect
  each scene across the three mask widths. Larger symbols show density-stratified
  means and 95\% bootstrap intervals.}
  \label{fig:width}
\end{figure}

Replacing the degraded one-pixel masks by their clean procedural axes produced
a small negative mean change whose paired interval included zero. At 30\%
coverage, all four available development scenes decreased, but the sample is
too small for a density-specific inference. Under this generator and
configuration, removing the modeled degradation did not produce a resolved
aggregate improvement. This is a conditional diagnostic, not evidence that real
segmentation errors are unimportant.

\end{figure}

\subsection{Real SEM development case study}
\label{sec:res-real}

The single $512\times512$ real crop contains 58 manually annotated filament
instances. It informed development and therefore provides neither a held-out
estimate nor a population confidence interval; every real-data result in this
paper comes from this one development crop
(Section~\ref{sec:limitations}). Figure~\ref{fig:qual-real} is
descriptive: predicted instances inherit the color of their one-to-one matched
manual instance, false predictions are magenta, and the one unmatched manual
instance is outlined in yellow. The final rendered instances are shown because
this panel illustrates the delivered output, not because the thick
representation is the primary grouping endpoint.
