\subsection{Which density axis matters}
\label{sec:res-factorial}

Coverage is the fraction of the frame filled by the rendered bundles, so it
rises both when filaments are added and when the same filaments are drawn
thicker, and only the first changes the grouping problem. A factorial separates
the two. Twelve geometries, three centreline length
densities crossed with four seeds, were each rendered at bundle widths of 6,
11 and 16 px. Within one geometry the input axis mask and the overlap-aware
centreline reference are byte-identical across the three widths, verified by
SHA-256, while the union of the thick supports changes: areal coverage varies by
up to a factor of \PlectaFactorialMaxCoverageRatio{}. Any movement in the score
across those three renderings would mean that the method or the metric reads the
silhouette.

Across all \PlectaFactorialGeometryCount{} geometries the within-geometry
\Fone{} range was \PlectaFactorialMaxFOneRange{}, that is, exactly zero in
\PlectaFactorialZeroRangeCount{} of \PlectaFactorialGeometryCount{}
(Table~\ref{tab:plecta-factorial}). Areal coverage therefore cannot causally
affect the grouping score; PLECTA reads the axis mask, and the metric scores
centreline fragments, so neither sees the width the bundles were drawn at.
Centreline length density does move it: mean \Fone{} fell from
\PlectaFactorialLowFOne{} to \PlectaFactorialHighFOne{} as centreline coverage
rose from \PlectaFactorialLowLengthDensity{} to
\PlectaFactorialHighLengthDensity{}. The coverage bands of the three length
densities also overlap, an areal coverage near 0.31 occurring at all three, so
coverage does not identify a difficulty level on its own.

Density strata reported by target areal coverage should therefore be read as a
proxy for the length density that accompanies them under this generator, and a
mask drawn at a different thickness, as an upstream segmenter may well produce,
does not by itself move the grouping score.

\subsection{Comparison with greedy continuation}
\label{sec:res-greedy}

The contribution claimed in Section~\ref{sec:intro} is that a junction is
better solved as one exact matching, with an explicit priced option to leave an
arm unmatched, than by pairing endpoints greedily in priority order, the rule
used by SIFNE, by GraFT's path cover, and by the continuity baseline of Wegmayr
et al.\ \citep{zhang2017sifne,osterlund2026graft,wegmayr2020microplastic}. The
comparator therefore skeletonises the same mask, prunes spurs, splits the
skeleton into branches, and greedily accepts the lowest-turning-angle pairing
between branch ends within a distance and a turning-angle gate. Both gates
were selected on the same \PlectaDevelopmentSceneCount{} development scenes
PLECTA was configured on, by sweeping a grid and taking the best mean
\Fone{}, so neither method is advantaged by tuning effort. Both received
identical masks and one scorer, on \PlectaGreedySceneCount{} scenes generated
after both configurations were fixed.

PLECTA scored \PlectaGreedyPlectaFOne{} against
\PlectaGreedySweptBaselineFOne{}, a paired difference of
\PlectaGreedySweptDeltaFOne{} (95\% CI [\PlectaGreedySweptDeltaCILow{},
\PlectaGreedySweptDeltaCIHigh{}]), and was better in
\PlectaGreedySweptWinCount{} of \PlectaGreedySceneCount{} scenes
(Table~\ref{tab:plecta-greedy}). Adjusted Rand index and variation of
information rank the two the same way, so the result does not depend on the
in-house measure. The gap is widest in recall and in reference-instance
recovery, the expected signature: a greedy pass commits to the locally
straightest continuation and cannot revise it when that choice strands a better
partner, so single filaments are left divided. On clean axes the difference was
\PlectaGreedySweptCleanDeltaFOne{}
[\PlectaGreedySweptCleanDeltaCILow{}, \PlectaGreedySweptCleanDeltaCIHigh{}], so it is not
an artefact of mask degradation.

\subsection{Comparison with a released implementation}
\label{sec:res-dnai}

The greedy rule above is a reimplementation, so we also ran a published method
from its own source. DNAi \citep{playout2026dnai} post-processes a binary
segmentation by the sequence described in Section~\ref{sec:intro}. Its
segmentation network was bypassed and only that stage was used, so grouping is
not confounded with segmentation quality, and both methods received the same
masks and the same scorer.

At its released defaults DNAi scored \Fone{}=\PlectaDNAiShippedFOne{} on the
degraded masks, which is not a fair measure of it. The cause is its
single-linkage clustering of junction pixels at 10~px: our crossings are closer
together than that, so the clustering chains, and at 60\% coverage the largest
cluster spans 89~px and is replaced by one small erasure disk, leaving the
crossings along it unseparated and the whole tangle a single instance. Reducing
the clustering distance to 2~px on development scenes raised development
\Fone{} from 0.211 to 0.378 and cut variation-of-information merge from 2.07 to
0.66, confirming the mechanism. Dilating the input to a thicker mask was also
tested across 41 development configurations and made DNAi worse at every
setting, so it was not used.

At its best development-selected configuration DNAi scored
\PlectaDNAiFOne{} against PLECTA's \PlectaDNAiPlectaFOne{}, a paired difference
of \PlectaDNAiDeltaFOne{} (95\% CI [\PlectaDNAiDeltaCILow{},
\PlectaDNAiDeltaCIHigh{}]) over \PlectaDNAiSceneCount{} scenes; on clean axes
the difference was \PlectaDNAiCleanDeltaFOne{}
[\PlectaDNAiCleanDeltaCILow{}, \PlectaDNAiCleanDeltaCIHigh{}]. The gap widens
monotonically with density (Figure~\ref{fig:comparators}). Both methods must be
read against the one-instance-per-connected-component floor at their own
density, since that floor is itself strongly density-dependent and a pooled
value would flatter any method at 60\% coverage.

\begin{figure}[htbp]
    \centering
    \includegraphics[width=\linewidth]{figures/fig_comparators_density.pdf}
    \caption{PLECTA, the greedy continuation rule and DNAi across coverage, on
    the same \PlectaGreedySceneCount{} scenes with identical masks and one
    scorer, for the degraded and clean axis conditions. Plain means, ten scenes
    per point, no interval and no inference. The connected-component floor is
    drawn per density and falls from 0.146 to 0.041 across this range. DNAi is
    shown at its released defaults and at the best configuration selected on
    development scenes.}
    \label{fig:comparators}
\end{figure}

This is a limitation of domain and density, not a general ranking. DNAi is
built for DNA-fibre micrographs, which are sparse: its own bundled test image
contains five junction clusters, whereas our scenes contain between 23 and 183,
and where the network is sparse and the axis clean it recovers
\PlectaDNAiCleanSparseFOne{} at 20\% coverage. Two structural differences
explain the rest. It commits to a pairing at every junction of degree two to
four with no cost threshold, so it cannot decline a continuation the way a
priced unmatched option can; and it resolves only junctions of degree at most
four. That cap is easy to underrate by counting junctions, since junctions of
degree five or more are only about a tenth of the clusters here. Weighted by the
evidence the metric actually counts, they carry
\PlectaEvidenceHighDegree{}\,\% of same-filament fragment pairs on the degraded
masks and \PlectaEvidenceCleanHighDegree{}\,\% on the clean ones, because a
single such junction severs every pair that spans it.

That raises the question of how much of the margin is capability rather than
judgement. Restricting the score to the evidence a degree-limited, gap-blind
method can reach answers it. On clean axes the difference falls from
\PlectaDNAiCleanDeltaFOne{} to \PlectaDNAiRestrictedCleanDeltaFOne{}
[\PlectaDNAiRestrictedCleanDeltaCILow{},
\PlectaDNAiRestrictedCleanDeltaCIHigh{}], still favouring PLECTA in
\PlectaDNAiRestrictedCleanWinCount{} of \PlectaDNAiCleanSceneCount{} scenes and
at every coverage level. DNAi gains far more from the restriction than PLECTA
does, which is what shows the restriction genuinely removes its handicap. So
roughly \PlectaDNAiCoverageShareClean{}\,\% of the margin is coverage of cases
the comparator cannot attempt and \PlectaDNAiDecisionShareClean{}\,\% is the
outcome of decisions both methods make. Consistent with that, after its
clustering is repaired DNAi's remaining errors are dominated by division rather
than merging, and a third of those divisions occur at junctions of degree four,
which it does attempt: its matcher commits to a pairing there with no rejection
threshold.

A second released method was run the same way. GraFT \citep{osterlund2026graft}
builds a graph on a skeleton, derives an angle threshold per component, and
traces filaments by constrained depth-first search followed by a greedy
longest-first path cover. Its published entry point begins with a Frangi and
Otsu front end that enhances and binarises grayscale ridge images, which has
nothing to do on a two-valued axis, so the mask was injected at its
skeleton-to-graph stage and everything downstream is its own code unchanged.
That decision was checked rather than assumed, and it favours GraFT: run end to
end on the grayscale images it scores \PlectaGraFTNativeFOne{}, near the
connectivity floor, because its segmentation stage recovers only a third to a
half of the reference skeleton. Its minimum continuation angle was re-tuned on
development scenes, gaining it a further 0.04.

GraFT scored \PlectaGraFTFOne{}, a paired difference of
\PlectaGraFTDeltaFOne{} [\PlectaGraFTDeltaCILow{}, \PlectaGraFTDeltaCIHigh{}]
favouring PLECTA in every one of the \PlectaGraFTSceneCount{} scenes both
methods completed, and \PlectaGraFTCleanDeltaFOne{}
[\PlectaGraFTCleanDeltaCILow{}, \PlectaGraFTCleanDeltaCIHigh{}] on clean axes.
Its errors are divisions rather than merges (variation of information split
\PlectaGraFTVISplit{} against merge \PlectaGraFTVIMerge{}), which follows from a
greedy cover that cannot revise an early commitment once the scene collapses
into one connected graph, as it does above 30\% coverage.
\PlectaGraFTFailed{} of the densest degraded scenes did not finish inside a
45-minute budget and are excluded from its means; they are the hardest scenes of
the set, so that exclusion flatters GraFT.
Section~\ref{sec:limitations} states what these comparisons leave untested.

\subsection{What the metric does not see}
\label{sec:res-overlap}

Every score in this paper uses the common-fragment metric, and one of its
properties has to be stated plainly. It splits the skeleton at junctions,
discards the junction pixels, and assigns each surviving fragment to exactly one
instance on both the reference and the prediction side. It is therefore blind to
whether an instance shares a crossing pixel.

Measured on the comparison set, reference instances share
\PlectaOverlapReferenceLow{}\,\% of their pixels with another instance at 20\%
coverage, rising to \PlectaOverlapReferenceHigh{}\,\% at 60\%. PLECTA expresses
\PlectaOverlapExpressedLow{}--\PlectaOverlapExpressedHigh{}\,\% of its pixels as
shared, and GraFT, whose output is close to a hard partition, about
\PlectaOverlapGraFTHigh{}\,\%. Yet flattening either output to a single label
image changes \Fone{} by at most \PlectaOverlapFlattenCost{} for PLECTA and
0.002 for GraFT.

Two things follow. GraFT's near-hard partition is a real structural limitation
that costs it nothing here, so its scores are not depressed by it and the
comparison above should be read accordingly. And the overlap-aware output this
paper describes is not what the headline number rewards: a method emitting a
flat partition of the same fragments would score the same. Shared crossing
pixels are a property of the representation PLECTA produces, evidenced by the
fractions above, rather than something the primary endpoint measures.
Establishing that crossing ownership is recovered \emph{correctly} requires a
metric that scores it directly, and that is left to future work.

Individual scenes show what the score corresponds to.
Figure~\ref{fig:examples} takes the scene whose \Fone{} is closest to the
median at three coverage levels, so the rows are typical rather than selected,
and shows the evaluated output beside the same instances after the optional
rendering stage. The grouping is scored on the centrelines in the third column;
the fourth adds the fitted widths and is what the full pipeline delivers. It is
included because the rendered form is what a downstream user sees, and because
the comparison with the reference is only legible once the instances are drawn
at width. Errors survive rendering unchanged: it redraws the instances the
grouping produced and cannot merge or divide them.

\subsection{Input-mask robustness}
\label{sec:res-mask}

On the separate \PlectaRobustnessSceneCount{}-scene controlled set,
mean \Fone{} was \PlectaRobustnessDegradedFOneMean{} from degraded axes and
\PlectaRobustnessCleanFOneMean{} from the corresponding clean axes. The paired
clean-minus-degraded change was \PlectaCleanMinusDegradedFOneMean{} (95\% CI
[\PlectaCleanMinusDegradedFOneCILow{},
\PlectaCleanMinusDegradedFOneCIHigh{}]). Modelled gaps and raster
irregularities therefore caused a small resolved loss under this generator.

Mask thickness costs more than degradation does (Figure~\ref{fig:sensitivity}),
which matters because it is the upstream segmenter, not the method, that fixes
that thickness. Each width variant is scored against common fragments rebuilt
from its own input mask, so the question asked is how well the method groups the
skeleton it is handed rather than how many pixels it was handed; the decline
with width is a thicker mask skeletonising into a messier graph, not a change in
the grouping problem.

\subsection{Development ablations and downstream rendering}
\label{sec:res-ablation}

One-at-a-time ablations were evaluated only on the
\PlectaDevelopmentSceneCount{} development scenes
(Table~\ref{tab:plecta-ablation}).
Relative to the evaluated configuration, removing the junction chord-turn term
changed \Fone{} by \PlectaAblationTangentTermOnlyDeltaFOne{}, removing gap
matching by \PlectaAblationNoGapBridgingDeltaFOne{}, and disabling
connector-based junction-cluster consolidation by
\PlectaAblationNoCrossingMergingAtAllDeltaFOne{}. Every other component,
chain-extended frames and the round schedule included, mattered less, the
largest such change being \PlectaAblationNoJoinPxDeltaFOne{}. Chord-aware
continuation, gap matching, and junction-topology preconditioning are thus the
largest tested contributors, but these are development values and not held-out
effect estimates.

The core output is a grouping, not a set of connected curves: accepted gap
links establish identity but are not painted, so on the development set
\PlectaCoreDisconnected{} of core instances arrive as more than one disjoint
pixel run and \PlectaCoreBranched{} contain a branch point. Any downstream step
that assumes one connected curve per instance must account for this. Default
Stage~4 ribbon rendering scored
development \Fone{}=\PlectaStageFourFOne{}, a change of
\PlectaStageFourDeltaFOne{}, and reduced those fractions to
\PlectaStageFourDisconnected{} and \PlectaStageFourBranched{}, so rendering buys
connectivity at a small cost in the identity score without changing chain
membership. Enabling the separate 0.6 silhouette-absorption rule reduced
\Fone{} further to \PlectaStageFourAbsorbFOne{}, and because absorption can
merge identities it remained disabled. None of these topology measurements
validates apparent width.

Two properties of the held-out result are invisible in a table of means
(Figure~\ref{fig:performance}). The first is the negative control of
Section~\ref{sec:res-factorial} seen scene by scene: within a fixed geometry the
twelve lines are flat, so the coverage axis of Figures~\ref{fig:density}
and~\ref{fig:comparators} stands in for centreline density rather than causing
the difficulty itself. The second is that PLECTA's errors are two-sided. A
fragment \Fone{} can be reached one-sidedly, by a method that merges everything
or one that divides everything, so it matters that PLECTA sits on the
precision--recall diagonal. The connected-component floor does not: it recovers
\PlectaCCFloorRecall{} of same-filament pairs at a precision of
\PlectaCCFloorPrecision{}, because it merges every filament of a connected
network into one instance. That placement is what makes it legible as a
diagnostic rather than a competitor.
