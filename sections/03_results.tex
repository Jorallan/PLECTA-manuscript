\subsection{Which density axis matters}
\label{sec:res-factorial}

Coverage is the fraction of the frame filled by the rendered bundles, so it
rises both when filaments are added and when the same filaments are drawn
thicker, and only the first changes the grouping problem. A factorial separates
them: twelve geometries, three centreline length densities crossed with four
seeds, were each rendered at bundle widths of 6, 11 and 16 px. Within one geometry the input axis mask and the overlap-aware
centreline reference are byte-identical across the three widths, verified by
SHA-256, while the union of the thick supports changes: areal coverage varies by
up to a factor of \PlectaFactorialMaxCoverageRatio{}. Any movement in the score
across those three renderings would mean that the method or the metric reads the
silhouette.

Across all \PlectaFactorialGeometryCount{} geometries the within-geometry
\Fone{} range was \PlectaFactorialMaxFOneRange{}, that is, exactly zero in
\PlectaFactorialZeroRangeCount{} of \PlectaFactorialGeometryCount{}
(Table~\ref{tab:plecta-factorial}). Areal coverage therefore cannot causally
affect the grouping score; PLECTA reads the axis mask, and the metric scores
centreline fragments, so neither sees the width the bundles were drawn at.
Centreline length density does move it, mean \Fone{} falling from
\PlectaFactorialLowFOne{} to \PlectaFactorialHighFOne{} across the three
densities of Table~\ref{tab:plecta-factorial}. The coverage bands of the three length
densities also overlap, so coverage does not identify a difficulty level on its
own. Density strata reported by target areal coverage should therefore be read
as a proxy for the length density accompanying them under this generator.

\subsection{Comparison with prior linkers}
\label{sec:res-comparators}

One baseline and two published methods were run on identical input masks with
one scorer. The baseline is a greedy minimum-turning-angle continuation rule,
and it is ours: not a reimplementation of any particular paper but of the
pairing principle several of them share, reported as the transparent floor the
claim of Section~\ref{sec:intro} is made against and never as an external
result. The published methods are DNAi \citep{playout2026dnai} and GraFT
\citep{osterlund2026graft}, each executed from its own released source. All were
scored through the same conversion and metric code as PLECTA, and that path was
gated on parity: before any comparator number was believed it had to reproduce
PLECTA's stored \Fone{}, adjusted Rand index and both variation-of-information
components, because a faulty conversion yields plausible rather than obviously
wrong scores. Each comparator received a parameter translation selected on the
same \PlectaDevelopmentSceneCount{} development scenes PLECTA was configured
on, and every adaptation below was checked and found to favour the comparator.

\textbf{Greedy continuation.} The contribution claimed in
Section~\ref{sec:intro} is that a junction is better solved as one exact
matching, with an explicit priced option to leave an arm unmatched, than by
pairing endpoints greedily in priority order, the rule used by SIFNE, by GraFT's
path cover, and by the continuity baseline of Wegmayr et al.\
\citep{zhang2017sifne,osterlund2026graft,wegmayr2020microplastic}. The
comparator therefore skeletonises the same mask, prunes spurs, splits the
skeleton into branches, and greedily accepts the lowest-turning-angle pairing
between branch ends within a distance and a turning-angle gate, both gates fixed
by a grid sweep for best mean development \Fone{}. On
\PlectaGreedySceneCount{} scenes generated after both configurations were fixed,
PLECTA scored \PlectaGreedyPlectaFOne{} against
\PlectaGreedySweptBaselineFOne{} and was better in
\PlectaGreedySweptWinCount{} of them; Table~\ref{tab:plecta-comparators} gives
that difference and every other, the field-standard measures ranking the two as
the in-house one does. On clean axes the
difference was \PlectaGreedySweptCleanDeltaFOne{}
[\PlectaGreedySweptCleanDeltaCILow{}, \PlectaGreedySweptCleanDeltaCIHigh{}], so
it is not an artefact of mask degradation.

\textbf{Exact against greedy matching, matcher only.} That baseline replaces
the whole linker; a second arm isolates the claim, keeping the graph, cost,
admissibility gate and round schedule and replacing only the per-junction
maximum-weight matching by greedy cheapest-first acceptance. Exactness was
worth \PlectaMatcherDeltaFOne{} \Fone{} [\PlectaMatcherDeltaCILow{},
\PlectaMatcherDeltaCIHigh{}] over \PlectaMatcherSceneCount{} development
scenes, on \PlectaMatcherWinCount{} of \PlectaMatcherSceneCount{} of them, and
\PlectaMatcherCleanDeltaFOne{} [\PlectaMatcherCleanDeltaCILow{},
\PlectaMatcherCleanDeltaCIHigh{}] on clean axes; the failure is division, not
merging, with variation-of-information split rising
\PlectaMatcherExactVISplit{} to \PlectaMatcherGreedyVISplit{} at unchanged
merge, \PlectaMatcherInstanceExcess{}\,\% more instances emitted and crossings
resolved exactly falling \PlectaMatcherExactJunctionRate{} to
\PlectaMatcherGreedyJunctionRate{}. The relative loss grows monotonically with
degree, \PlectaMatcherDegreeThreeRelative{}\,\% at degree three to
\PlectaMatcherDegreeSixRelative{}\,\% at degree six, but in absolute terms
peaks at degree four (\PlectaMatcherDegreeFourAbsolute{}), where most crossings
lie; and the greedy arm runs at parameters selected with the exact matcher in
the loop, a bias in that matcher's favour.


\textbf{DNAi.} DNAi post-processes a binary segmentation by the sequence
described in Section~\ref{sec:intro}; its segmentation network was bypassed and
only that stage used, so grouping is not confounded with segmentation quality.
At its released defaults it scored \Fone{}=\PlectaDNAiShippedFOne{} on the
degraded masks, which is not a fair measure of it: its single-linkage clustering
of junction pixels at 10~px chains together crossings that lie closer than that,
and at 60\% coverage the largest cluster spans 89~px and is replaced by one
small erasure disk, leaving the whole tangle a single instance. Reducing that
distance to 2~px on development scenes raised development \Fone{} from 0.211 to
0.378 and cut variation-of-information merge from 2.07 to 0.66, confirming the
mechanism. At its best development-selected configuration DNAi scored
\PlectaDNAiFOne{}, a paired difference of
\PlectaDNAiDeltaFOne{} [\PlectaDNAiDeltaCILow{}, \PlectaDNAiDeltaCIHigh{}] over
\PlectaDNAiSceneCount{} scenes, and \PlectaDNAiCleanDeltaFOne{}
[\PlectaDNAiCleanDeltaCILow{}, \PlectaDNAiCleanDeltaCIHigh{}] on clean axes,
the gap widening monotonically with density
(Figures~\ref{fig:panels-degraded} and~\ref{fig:panels-clean}).
Both methods must be read against the one-instance-per-connected-component floor
at their own density, since that floor is itself strongly density-dependent and
a pooled value would flatter any method at 60\% coverage.

That margin is a limitation of domain and density, not a general ranking. DNAi
is built for DNA-fibre micrographs, which are sparse: its own bundled test image
contains five junction clusters, whereas our scenes contain between 23 and 183,
and where the network is sparse and the axis clean it recovers
\PlectaDNAiCleanSparseFOne{} at 20\% coverage. Two structural differences
explain the rest. It commits to a pairing at every junction of degree two to
four with no cost threshold, so it cannot decline a continuation the way a
priced unmatched option can; and it resolves only junctions of degree at most
four, measurably so, resolving \PlectaJunctionDNAiDegreeFivePlus{} of the
crossings above that degree. Those are about a tenth of the crossings but carry
\PlectaEvidenceHighDegree{}\,\% of same-filament fragment pairs on the degraded
masks, one such junction severing every pair that spans it.

Restricting the score to the evidence a degree-limited, gap-blind method can
reach separates capability from judgement. On clean axes the difference falls
from \PlectaDNAiCleanDeltaFOne{} to \PlectaDNAiRestrictedCleanDeltaFOne{}
[\PlectaDNAiRestrictedCleanDeltaCILow{},
\PlectaDNAiRestrictedCleanDeltaCIHigh{}], still favouring PLECTA in
\PlectaDNAiRestrictedCleanWinCount{} of \PlectaDNAiCleanSceneCount{} scenes and
at every coverage level, DNAi gaining far more from the restriction than PLECTA
does. Roughly \PlectaDNAiCoverageShareClean{}\,\% of the margin is therefore
coverage of cases the comparator cannot attempt and
\PlectaDNAiDecisionShareClean{}\,\% is the outcome of decisions both methods
make.

\textbf{GraFT.} GraFT builds a graph on a skeleton, derives an angle threshold
per component, and traces filaments by constrained depth-first search followed
by a greedy longest-first path cover. Its published entry point begins with a
Frangi and Otsu front end for grayscale ridge images, which has nothing to do
on a two-valued axis, so the mask was injected at its skeleton-to-graph stage,
everything downstream being its own code unchanged.
That substitution favours GraFT: run end to end on the grayscale images it
scores \PlectaGraFTNativeFOne{}, near the connectivity floor, because its
segmentation stage recovers only a third to a half of the reference skeleton.
Its minimum continuation angle was re-tuned on development scenes, gaining it a
further 0.04. GraFT scored \PlectaGraFTFOne{}, a paired difference of
\PlectaGraFTDeltaFOne{} [\PlectaGraFTDeltaCILow{}, \PlectaGraFTDeltaCIHigh{}]
favouring PLECTA in every one of the \PlectaGraFTSceneCount{} scenes both
methods completed, and \PlectaGraFTCleanDeltaFOne{}
[\PlectaGraFTCleanDeltaCILow{}, \PlectaGraFTCleanDeltaCIHigh{}] on clean axes.
Its errors are divisions rather than merges (variation of information split
\PlectaGraFTVISplit{} against merge \PlectaGraFTVIMerge{}), which follows from a
greedy cover that cannot revise an early commitment once the scene collapses
into one connected graph, as it does above 30\% coverage.
\PlectaGraFTFailed{} of the densest degraded scenes did not finish inside a
45-minute budget and are excluded from its means; they are the hardest scenes of
the set, so that exclusion flatters GraFT. Neither released method establishes a
general ranking, and Section~\ref{sec:limitations} states what these comparisons
leave untested.

\textbf{On GraFT's own ground.} Every scene above comes from one generator,
which Section~\ref{sec:limitations} names as this paper's principal limitation,
so the same question was put to GraFT's own. Its repository bundles a single
\PlectaGraFTFrameSide{}$\times$\PlectaGraFTFrameSide{}~px synthetic frame with
per-filament ground truth, and through the identical pipeline PLECTA reached
\Fone{}=\PlectaGraFTFramePlectaFOne{} on it against
\PlectaGraFTFrameGraFTNativeFOne{} for GraFT run end to end on its own noisy
image; the instructive detail is that GraFT resolved
\PlectaGraFTFrameGraFTInjectedSolved{} of the frame's
\PlectaGraFTFrameCrossingCount{} crossings exactly while scoring
\Fone{}=\PlectaGraFTFrameGraFTInjectedFOne{}, so its errors there lie along
filaments rather than at crossings, which is why the two measures are reported
together. One frame is an anecdote, so GraFT's generator was then run unmodified
at its published settings over \PlectaRegimeSceneCount{} scenes in
\PlectaRegimeStratumCount{} density strata. The ladder spans
\PlectaRegimeDensityLow{} to \PlectaRegimeDensityHigh{} centreline pixels per
image pixel: GraFT's published comparison reaches
\PlectaRegimeDensityPublished{}, so its whole range is the lower half of this
one and this paper's own scenes occupy the upper half. On the endpoint used
throughout this paper PLECTA leads at every density, from
\PlectaRegimeLowPlectaFOne{} against \PlectaRegimeLowGraFTFOne{} and
\PlectaRegimeLowDNAiFOne{} at the sparsest, to
\PlectaRegimeHighPlectaFOne{} against \PlectaRegimeHighGraFTFOne{} and
\PlectaRegimeHighDNAiFOne{} at the densest; the margin widens with density and
nothing crosses over, including inside GraFT's published range. The
field-standard measures order them identically over the whole ladder, adjusted
Rand index \PlectaRegimeOverallPlectaARI{}, \PlectaRegimeOverallGraFTARI{} and
\PlectaRegimeOverallDNAiARI{} against variation of information
\PlectaRegimeOverallPlectaVITotal{}, \PlectaRegimeOverallGraFTVITotal{} and
\PlectaRegimeOverallDNAiVITotal{} bits, and the split and merge components
separate what the totals cannot: PLECTA's errors lean mildly to fusion,
\PlectaRegimeOverallPlectaVISplit{} against
\PlectaRegimeOverallPlectaVIMerge{}, while both comparators divide far more than
they fuse, \PlectaRegimeOverallGraFTVISplit{} against
\PlectaRegimeOverallGraFTVIMerge{} for GraFT and
\PlectaRegimeOverallDNAiVISplit{} against \PlectaRegimeOverallDNAiVIMerge{} for
DNAi. GraFT was also run end to end on the noisy images with its own front end,
which on this data is inside its design envelope rather than degenerate as it is
on ours; that arm scored \PlectaRegimeLowGraFTNativeFOne{} and
\PlectaRegimeHighGraFTNativeFOne{} at the two ends, below the injected arm
almost everywhere, so injecting the mask continues to favour it.

The same predictions score differently under GraFT's own filament matched
coverage, which asks whether each reference filament was matched one-to-one to
some detection, and the disagreement is worth stating rather than resolving. At
the densest stratum it reads \PlectaRegimeHighGraFTMatched{} for GraFT,
\PlectaRegimeHighDNAiMatched{} for DNAi and \PlectaRegimeHighPlectaMatched{} for
PLECTA --- the reverse order --- because it counts objects, so a method emitting
more objects than the scene contains has a spare for every filament: across the
whole ladder each method's matched coverage is the smaller of one and its
emission ratio to within \PlectaRegimeCoverageCountResidual{}. Requiring in
addition that a detection cover half the axis it claims, and changing nothing
else, restores the order: \PlectaRegimeHighPlectaMatchedHalf{} for PLECTA
against \PlectaRegimeHighGraFTMatchedHalf{} and
\PlectaRegimeHighDNAiMatchedHalf{}. The measure is not wrong; it asks whether
each filament was found, and a method that severs every filament at its
crossings still offers a distinct detection for each one.

\textbf{Crossings resolved.} Panel~(c) of Figures~\ref{fig:panels-degraded}
and~\ref{fig:panels-clean} states the contribution directly, and pooled over the
\PlectaJunctionCount{} reference crossings of the degraded set PLECTA
partitions \PlectaJunctionPlectaRate{} of them exactly against
\PlectaJunctionGreedyRate{} for the greedy baseline,
\PlectaJunctionGraFTRate{} for GraFT and \PlectaJunctionDNAiRate{} for DNAi;
paired, PLECTA's margins are \PlectaJunctionGreedyDelta{}
[\PlectaJunctionGreedyDeltaCILow{}, \PlectaJunctionGreedyDeltaCIHigh{}],
\PlectaJunctionGraFTDelta{} [\PlectaJunctionGraFTDeltaCILow{},
\PlectaJunctionGraFTDeltaCIHigh{}] and \PlectaJunctionDNAiDelta{}
[\PlectaJunctionDNAiDeltaCILow{}, \PlectaJunctionDNAiDeltaCIHigh{}]. Clean axes
order them the same way, PLECTA at \PlectaJunctionCleanPlectaRate{}. Leaving
every arm unpaired resolves \PlectaJunctionChance{} of these crossings, which
is the chance level the rate is read against. Two things about it should be
stated rather than left to be found. It is much less discriminative than the
pairwise score --- at 60\,\% coverage it spans
\PlectaJunctionSpreadHigh{} between best and worst method where pairwise
\Fone{} spans \PlectaPairwiseSpreadHigh{} --- so it is used to say what was
solved and not to rank. And it shares its assignment thresholds with the
common-fragment metric, so it is a sharper view of the same evidence rather
than independent confirmation of it. Everyone is poor at the busiest nodes:
above degree four PLECTA resolves \PlectaJunctionPlectaDegreeFivePlus{} and no
comparator does better, while DNAi collapses to
\PlectaJunctionDNAiDegreeFivePlus{}, which is what its degree-four cap
predicts.

Per-scene cost was measured warm, single-threaded and pinned to one core, timing
each method\'s own computation only (Table~\ref{tab:plecta-comparators};
\RuntimeGraftCovSixtyCensored{} GraFT scenes exceeded \RuntimeBudgetSeconds{}~s
and are censored). These are independently optimised codebases, so absolute
times measure implementation as much as algorithm; the transferable quantity is
scaling, and the log--log exponents against foreground pixels are
\RuntimeDnaiScalingExponent{}, \RuntimePlectaScalingExponent{} and at least
\RuntimeGraftScalingExponent{}. PLECTA grows fastest, but only GraFT is
heavy-tailed.

\subsection{Crossing representation, and where it over-marks}
\label{sec:res-overlap}

Resolving a crossing and representing one are different things, and only the
first is what the scores above measure. Whether a crossing is \emph{resolved}
correctly --- of the arms meeting at a junction, which continues into which ---
is the difficult decision, and every score in this paper rewards getting it
right. Whether the emitted raster \emph{represents} a crossing pixel as
belonging to both filaments is a property of the output format, and the metric
cannot see it: flattening either method's layers to a single label image changes
\Fone{} by at most \PlectaOverlapFlattenCost{} for PLECTA and 0.002 for GraFT.

The representation is instead verifiable directly: on the comparison set,
reference instances share \PlectaOverlapReferenceLow{}\,\% of their pixels with
another instance at 20\% coverage, rising to
\PlectaOverlapReferenceHigh{}\,\% at 60\%; PLECTA emits
\PlectaOverlapExpressedLow{}--\PlectaOverlapExpressedHigh{}\,\%, and GraFT,
whose output is close to a hard partition, about
\PlectaOverlapGraFTHigh{}\,\%. Which pixels those are is less flattering.
PLECTA recovers \PlectaSharedRecall{} of the pixels the reference gives to more
than one instance, the only method here that recovers most of them, but at a
precision of \PlectaSharedPrecision{}: it marks
\PlectaSharedExcess{}--\PlectaSharedCleanExcess{} times more shared pixels than
the reference contains, because a chain is painted with every pixel of every
junction cluster it touches --- a property of the rendering, not of the
grouping, and one the \Fone{} neither rewards nor penalises.

Figure~\ref{fig:examples} shows what the score corresponds to scene by scene,
taking at three coverage levels the scene whose \Fone{} is closest to the
median, so the rows are typical rather than selected.

\subsection{Input-mask robustness}
\label{sec:res-mask}

On the separate \PlectaRobustnessSceneCount{}-scene controlled set,
mean \Fone{} was \PlectaRobustnessDegradedFOneMean{} from degraded axes and
\PlectaRobustnessCleanFOneMean{} from the corresponding clean axes. The paired
clean-minus-degraded change was \PlectaCleanMinusDegradedFOneMean{} (95\% CI
[\PlectaCleanMinusDegradedFOneCILow{},
\PlectaCleanMinusDegradedFOneCIHigh{}]). Modelled gaps and raster
irregularities therefore caused a small resolved loss under this generator.

The thickness of the axis mask costs more, and it matters because it is the
upstream segmenter, not the method, that fixes it: over
\PlectaSensitivityWidthSceneCount{} development scenes a nominal
\PlectaSensitivityWidthHigh{}\,px axis mask scored
\PlectaSensitivityWidthDeltaFOne{} \Fone{}
[\PlectaSensitivityWidthDeltaCILow{}, \PlectaSensitivityWidthDeltaCIHigh{}]
against the \PlectaSensitivityWidthLow{}\,px mask of the same scenes. Each width
variant is scored against common fragments rebuilt from its own input mask, so
the question asked is how well the method groups the skeleton it is handed, and
the decline with width is a thicker mask skeletonising into a messier graph
rather than a change in the grouping problem. This is the one density axis that
does move the score. The rendered silhouette does not, causally and scene by
scene (Section~\ref{sec:res-factorial}), so a mask should be reported by the
geometry of its axis and not by how much of the frame its bundles fill.

The core output is a grouping, not a set of connected curves: accepted gap
links establish identity but are not painted, so on the development set
\PlectaCoreDisconnected{} of core instances arrive as more than one disjoint
pixel run and \PlectaCoreBranched{} contain a branch point. Any downstream step
that assumes one connected curve per instance must account for this. Default
Stage~4 ribbon rendering scored
development \Fone{}=\PlectaStageFourFOne{}, a change of
\PlectaStageFourDeltaFOne{}, and reduced those fractions to
\PlectaStageFourDisconnected{} and \PlectaStageFourBranched{}, so rendering buys
connectivity at a small cost in the identity score without changing chain
membership. Enabling the separate 0.6 silhouette-absorption rule reduced
\Fone{} further to \PlectaStageFourAbsorbFOne{}, and because absorption can
merge identities it remained disabled. None of these topology measurements
validates apparent width.

PLECTA's errors are two-sided, where a fragment \Fone{} can be reached
one-sidedly: it sits on the precision--recall diagonal, whereas the
connected-component floor recovers \PlectaCCFloorRecall{} of same-filament
pairs at a precision of only \PlectaCCFloorPrecision{}.

\subsection{An example of CNT SEM decomposition}
\label{sec:res-real}
\label{sec:res-qualitative}

Reaching a real micrograph takes both pipelines, and the order matters because
every number below depends on which stage produced what. Pipeline~A runs
procedural mask $\rightarrow$ CycleGAN $\rightarrow$ paired synthetic corpus
$\rightarrow$ nnU-Net $\rightarrow$ binary filament-axis mask: a generator draws
a mask, a CycleGAN translates it into an SEM-like image so that image and mask
are paired without an annotator, an nnU-Net trained on those pairs segments a
real micrograph, and its output is a binary axis mask
\citep{ruehle2021workflow,allan2026cntsem}. Pipeline~B is PLECTA, and it is the
only stage evaluated here. Three manually annotated CNT-sheet fields separate
the last two: PLECTA is run once on the skeleton of the manual annotation, an
oracle-input condition asking what the grouping can do, and once on the nnU-Net
axis, asking what the mask allows it to do. The annotation is the reference in
both cases, so the two conditions differ only in the mask.

\textbf{Which fields are held out, and what the exception costs.} B58\_110 and
B58\_300 are absent from the training set of the inference run used here,
verified by image content rather than by filename at tile correlations of 0.10
and 0.15, so both are genuine held-out observations of mask transfer.
B58\_100's image is present in every available training run at 0.9999 or above
and cannot be made otherwise; its U-Net row is an upper bound and is marked as
such in Table~\ref{tab:plecta-real-masks}. What that costs is measured rather
than left vague. Running \PlectaContaminationModelCount{} nnU-Net models over
all three fields scores the same field under models that held it out and one
that did not: on B58\_110 the two clean models give
\PlectaContaminationBFiveEightOneOneZeroCleanLow{} and
\PlectaContaminationBFiveEightOneOneZeroCleanHigh{} against
\PlectaContaminationBFiveEightOneOneZeroTrained{}, on B58\_300
\PlectaContaminationBFiveEightThreeZeroZeroCleanLow{} and
\PlectaContaminationBFiveEightThreeZeroZeroCleanHigh{} against
\PlectaContaminationBFiveEightThreeZeroZeroTrained{}. Training on a field is
worth \PlectaContaminationDeltaLow{} to \PlectaContaminationDeltaHigh{} pairwise
\Fone{}, the trained-on model predicting about a third more foreground, which is
the mechanism. On B58\_100 every model trained on the field, the three agree,
and there is no gap to see: that is the control.

\textbf{Most of the distance between the two conditions is the mask.} The axis
each field hands PLECTA is characterised by the tolerant
completeness/correctness/quality triple standard for thin structures, whose
third member is also GraFT's published Jaccard index in its intended reading. At
the \PlectaPlacementTolerance{}\,px tolerance used throughout, quality is
\PlectaMaskQualityBFiveEightOneZeroZeroQualityTwo{},
\PlectaMaskQualityBFiveEightOneOneZeroQualityTwo{} and
\PlectaMaskQualityBFiveEightThreeZeroZeroQualityTwo{}, over completeness
\PlectaBFiveEightOneZeroZeroAxisRecall{},
\PlectaBFiveEightOneOneZeroAxisRecall{} and
\PlectaBFiveEightThreeZeroZeroAxisRecall{} and correctness
\PlectaBFiveEightOneZeroZeroAxisPrecision{},
\PlectaBFiveEightOneOneZeroAxisPrecision{} and
\PlectaBFiveEightThreeZeroZeroAxisPrecision{}. No reconstruction is opened to
compute any of them: they describe the input. The tolerance is not a formality,
and reporting the triple without saying so would mislead. Quality falls to
\PlectaMaskQualityBFiveEightOneZeroZeroQualityOne{},
\PlectaMaskQualityBFiveEightOneOneZeroQualityOne{} and
\PlectaMaskQualityBFiveEightThreeZeroZeroQualityOne{} at 1\,px, because the
manual annotation and the nnU-Net output are two independent readings of the
same filaments and disagree in placement almost everywhere. On the synthetic
degraded axis, where the evaluated mask is derived from the reference rather
than read from the image a second time, the same measure is nearly flat above
1\,px (\PlectaMaskQualitySyntheticOne{} against
\PlectaMaskQualitySyntheticThree{} over
\PlectaMaskQualitySyntheticSceneCount{} scenes) and collapses to
\PlectaMaskQualitySyntheticZero{} at zero tolerance, where it reports raster
placement rather than geometry.

\textbf{The placement tolerance is a correction, and the control says so.}
Detection \Fone{} pairs whole predicted objects with whole reference objects by
overlap, and two independently read one-pixel centrelines two pixels apart
intersect in nothing: at zero tolerance it reads
\PlectaDetectionBFiveEightOneZeroZeroUNetStrict{} and
\PlectaDetectionBFiveEightOneOneZeroUNetStrict{} on the nnU-Net axes, against
\PlectaDetectionBFiveEightOneZeroZeroUNetTolerant{} and
\PlectaDetectionBFiveEightOneOneZeroUNetTolerant{} at
\PlectaPlacementTolerance{}\,px. On the manual-derived axes, where the input
mask is a skeleton of the annotation itself, the tolerance moves the same
measure by 0.000 --- there is nothing displaced there to forgive, and that is
what makes this a correction rather than an inflation. Centreline Dice
\citep{shit2021cldice}, which the thin-structure benchmark of
\citet{mais2024fisbe} substitutes for overlap in exactly this situation, does
not avoid the problem: it tests each side's skeleton against the other's
\emph{volume}, and a centreline grouping has none, so it falls the same way
(\PlectaClDiceBFiveEightOneZeroZeroUNetStrict{} against
\PlectaClDiceBFiveEightOneZeroZeroManualStrict{}) and needs the same width
allowance to recover.

\textbf{Under a looser mask the method fails by conservatism.} The pairwise
score is quadratic in fragments, so a filament the nnU-Net mask breaks into
several pieces carries every one of its pairs. Join \Fone{} counts one decision
per pair of fragment ends the mask brings within the
\PlectaRealJoinReach{}\,px this method will bridge, and reports how many
decisions each mask posed:
\PlectaJoinBFiveEightOneOneZeroManualDecisions{} on B58\_110's manual axis
against \PlectaJoinBFiveEightOneOneZeroUNetDecisions{} on its nnU-Net one. It
narrows the gap without closing it, and its decomposition says what the rest is:
a join precision of \PlectaJoinBFiveEightOneOneZeroUNetPrecision{} against a
recall of \PlectaJoinBFiveEightOneOneZeroUNetRecall{} is a method declining
joins, not fusing filaments.
