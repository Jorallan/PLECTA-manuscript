\section{Instance segmentation: cost function, gates and schedule}
\label{supp:recon}

This section gives the numerical detail of Section~\ref*{M-sec:mm-plecta} of
the article. All values are those of the released configuration and were
selected on development data only.

\subsection{Skeleton graph}

The mask $M$ is binarised at $>0$ and skeletonised, and spurs no longer than
\PlectaParamSpurPx{}\,px are removed iteratively. Skeleton pixels of degree at
least three form junction clusters, and adjacent clusters merge into nodes:
connectors up to \PlectaParamAbsorbPx{}\,px are absorbed into one node,
connectors up to \PlectaParamJoinPx{}\,px merge nearby nodes while remaining
arms, and free debris up to \PlectaParamFreeDebrisPx{}\,px is absorbed. Each
remaining connected path is stored as an ordered arm with one stub at each
end.

\subsection{Stub frames}

A stub frame consists of the tip position, the outward tangent and the signed
curvature, estimated by a least-squares polynomial fit in arclength measured
outward from the tip. The fit is quadratic when the span reaches
\PlectaParamMinQuadratic{}\,px and linear otherwise, and a frame is considered
reliable only if it rests on at least three samples spanning at least
\PlectaParamSpurPx{}\,px. Local frames use \PlectaParamWindowLocal{}\,px of
support. Once links form chains, the support extends to
\PlectaParamWindowChain{}\,px, so that a stub too short to define a direction
of its own inherits one from the chain it has joined.

\subsection{Weights and scales of the pairing cost}

Junction and gap links use separate weight sets
(Table~\ref{tab:cost-weights}). A junction link is scored on direction alone,
whereas a gap link is also charged for the distance it spans; junction links
therefore set $w_\ell=0$. The curvature term carries a constant of
\PlectaParamKappaScale{}, which brings curvature, in px$^{-1}$, onto the scale
of the angular terms. The parameters $\ell_0$ and $d_0$ set the length scale of
the gap penalty and the width of the chord fade, respectively.

\subsection{Penalties, gates and admissibility}

Leaving a stub unmatched incurs a penalty $p_i$ rather than being prohibited.
Exact maximum-weight partial matching maximises $\sum(p_i+p_j-C_{ij})$ over
disjoint pairs, which is equivalent to minimising the total link cost plus
$p_i$ for every stub left unmatched. The same penalty determines
admissibility, $C_{ij}<p_i+p_j$. This is not an additional threshold: an edge
with $C_{ij}\ge p_i+p_j$ makes no positive contribution to the objective and
would never be selected by a partial matching.

Junction stubs carry $p_i=\PlectaParamJunctionPrice{}$, so a junction edge
requires $C<\PlectaParamJunctionLimit{}$. Gap stubs carry
$p_i=\PlectaParamGapPrice{}$, so a gap edge requires $C<\PlectaParamGapLimit{}$
and, in addition, $d\le\PlectaParamGapMaxLen{}$\,px,
$\theta\le\PlectaParamGapMaxTheta{}$\,rad and
$\varphi\le\PlectaParamGapMaxPhi{}$\,rad at each end. The gap gates are
stricter because a gap link asserts continuity across missing, rather than
ambiguous, evidence. All values were fixed on development scenes.

\subsection{Round schedule}

Rounds $r=0,\ldots,\PlectaParamRounds{}-1$ apply a schedule fraction
$\alpha_r=\PlectaParamAnnealStart{}+(1-\PlectaParamAnnealStart{})\,r/7$ to the
unmatched penalties and to the two gap angle limits, and to nothing else. A
lower penalty admits fewer edges and a lower angle limit narrows the gap gates,
so the early rounds, in which frame estimates are least reliable, are
deliberately conservative; the constraints relax as the assembled chains
improve the frame estimates. Only the final round, $\alpha_7=1$, runs at the
configured parameters, and its result is returned. The complete procedure is:
\begin{enumerate}[nosep,leftmargin=1.5em,label=\arabic*.]
  \item Skeletonise $M$, prune spurs, merge junction clusters into nodes, and
        store the remaining paths as arms with a stub at each end.
  \item For $r=0,\ldots,7$, re-solving both matchings from scratch and carrying
        only the chain structure forward from round $r-1$:
  \begin{enumerate}[nosep,leftmargin=1.5em,label=(\alph*)]
    \item Re-fit every stub frame along the chain to which it now belongs.
    \item At each node, form candidate edges between stubs of different arms
          with finite frames and cost, retain those admissible at $\alpha_r$,
          and solve one maximum-weight matching per node.
    \item From the still-unmatched reliable stubs, form gap candidates between
          different arms and different nodes that pass the distance gate and
          the $\alpha_r$-scaled angle gates, and solve one global
          maximum-weight matching over the admissible candidates.
  \end{enumerate}
  \item Return the links of round $r=7$.
\end{enumerate}

\subsection{Joint versus sequential matching}

Joint and sequential solutions coincide at simple crossings and diverge only
at dense nodes. Over 20 development scenes they never differed at nodes of
degree 2--4 ($n=542$), differed at 22\,\% of nodes with 5--8 stubs ($n=187$)
and at 69\,\% of nodes with 9 or more ($n=233$); at these nodes the joint
solution had a lower total cost, by a median of 0.90. This comparison holds
the costs of a single node fixed. Within the round schedule, where each
matching determines the frames against which the next is scored, an early
low-cost choice alters later frame estimates, so the difference is not
confined to dense nodes.

\subsection{Instance output}

Connected components of accepted arm links define instances. Every arm pixel,
and every junction cluster touched by a chain, is assigned to that chain's
instance, so intersecting instances share crossing pixels. Isolated one-arm
components shorter than 6\,px are omitted. Accepted gap links determine
identity but are not drawn into the centreline output. All effective
parameters, including former code defaults, are stated explicitly in the
released JSON configuration.

\section{Depth stage: evidence and solver}
\label{supp:depthstage}

This section gives the detail of Section~\ref*{M-sec:mm-depth} of the
article.

\subsection{Sampling geometry at a crossing}

Where two instance centrelines intersect, a short arc of each is sampled
through the crossing: the shared \emph{core}, a fixed \PlectaParamCorePx{}\,px
of arc at every crossing, and the \emph{flanks} on either side of it. Flank
samples are kept clear of the other strand, because a sample inside it would
measure the superposition of both strands rather than one. A crossing with too
few usable flank samples abstains. Earlier versions sized the core from the
radii of the two strands; with a fixed core, no radius estimate enters the
evidence, and radius errors cannot affect a depth decision. Radii enter only
downstream, in the non-interpenetration constraints that determine metric
height.

\subsection{Intensity score and noise floor}

A single quantity, the median intensity, is read on the core and on the
flanks. Let $m_i$ and $m_j$ denote the flank medians of the two strands and
$m_c$ the pooled core median. The evidence at the crossing is the score
\begin{equation}
  s \;=\; 2\,
  \frac{\lvert m_c-m_j\rvert-\lvert m_c-m_i\rvert}
       {\max\!\bigl(\lvert m_i-m_j\rvert,\;2\hat\sigma\bigr)},
  \label{eq:depth-score}
\end{equation}
whose sign identifies the upper strand, namely the one whose flanks the core
resembles more closely, and whose magnitude $\lvert s\rvert$ is the weight of
that decision. A crossing abstains when
$\lvert s\rvert<\PlectaParamAbstainScore{}$, a threshold defined directly in
score units. The noise estimate $\hat\sigma$ is computed per crossing as
$1.4826$ times the median absolute deviation of the within-strand flank
residuals, obtained by centring each strand's flank samples on their own
median and pooling the two residual sets. It therefore measures the variation
within each strand's appearance, not the difference between the two strands.
For compatibility with the output format, each crossing record also contains a
probability $p$; this is a monotone transformation of $s$, not a calibrated
confidence, and is not used in any decision or weighting.

\textbf{Noise floor in the denominator.} By the reverse triangle inequality,
the numerator of \eqref{eq:depth-score} is bounded in magnitude by
$\lvert m_i-m_j\rvert$, so a denominator equal to that separation drives the
score to $\pm1$ whenever the two strands differ at all. A previous version of
the rule bounded the denominator below by a small fixed constant in grey
levels, well below the noise level of these images. A maximal score could then
arise from a separation smaller than the noise, and because a maximal score
exceeds any abstention threshold, such crossings could never abstain. This
case was common: a substantial fraction of crossings saturated in this way,
and those whose separation was below the noise level were markedly less
accurate than those separated by more. Bounding the denominator by
$2\hat\sigma$ ensures that a high score indicates a separation larger than the
noise, and allows the stage to abstain where the evidence is weak.

\textbf{Removed gradient channel.} An earlier version also read the median
gradient magnitude along each strand's two edges, at centreline
$\pm r\,\mathbf{n}$, on the premise that the upper strand's edges remain sharp
through the crossing while those of the lower strand are occluded. On real
crossings this channel decided almost no crossings, agreed with the intensity
channel no better than chance, and was itself at chance level. Because the
channel is not part of the released implementation, these measurements are not
reported quantitatively. The cause lies in the sampling geometry: within the
core, the $\pm r$ offsets fall inside the other strand, so the measurement is
contaminated exactly where it is needed. Removing the channel also removed the
gradient image, the two channel weights and the logistic function that
combined the channels; with a single channel, $s$ is itself the decision
variable.

\textbf{Comparison with the two-channel rule.} The one- and two-channel rules
were compared on \PlectaDepthRuleHeldoutScenes{} optically rendered synthetic
scenes containing \PlectaDepthRuleHeldoutCrossings{} crossings. The core
half-size (\PlectaParamCorePx{}\,px) was chosen by a sweep on the same set, and
the insensitivity of the result to the abstention threshold was also checked
on it, so the comparison is in-sample with respect to these two values. Both
values were nevertheless inherited rather than newly selected:
\PlectaParamCorePx{}\,px is the value the previous rule's radius-free fallback
already used, and both lie on a plateau. A fixed core performs no worse than
oracle per-strand radii anywhere between 6 and 14\,px, and accuracy is
unchanged over a band of thresholds around \PlectaParamAbstainScore{}.

At matched coverage, with both rules answering as often as the released rule
does at \PlectaDepthRuleCovShipped{}\,\%, the one-channel rule matches the
accuracy of the two-channel rule, with a difference of
$\PlectaDepthRuleAccDelta{}$ (95\,\% CI $\PlectaDepthRuleAccCI{}$). When both
rules are forced to decide every crossing, including those on which they would
abstain, the one-channel rule is less accurate by
$\PlectaDepthRuleAccDeltaFull{}$ (CI $\PlectaDepthRuleAccFullCI{}$); neither
rule is operated in this mode. The one-channel rule orders its confidence
better: the area under the curve of correctness against $\lvert s\rvert$
increases from $\PlectaDepthRuleAucOld{}$ to $\PlectaDepthRuleAucNew{}$, a gain
of $\PlectaDepthRuleAucDelta{}$ (CI $\PlectaDepthRuleAucCI{}$). Because
$\lvert s\rvert$ is the edge weight of the global ordering described below,
better-ordered confidence means that contradictions are resolved at the
weakest relations. It also translates into higher accuracy when more
abstention is acceptable, by $\PlectaDepthRuleAccAtCovA{}$ at
\PlectaDepthRuleCovAPct{}\,\% coverage and by $\PlectaDepthRuleAccAtCovB{}$ at
\PlectaDepthRuleCovBPct{}\,\%. The one-channel rule therefore matches the
accuracy of the two-channel rule, with better-ordered confidence and a simpler
evidence path.

\subsection{Global ordering}

Each decided crossing contributes a directed edge, oriented according to the
sign of $s$ and weighted by $\lvert s\rvert$; $p$ is not used. Within each
connected component of the crossing graph, a total order of the instances is
derived that maximises the total weight of satisfied relations, and every
relation in the component is then re-read from this order. Relations that
disagree with the order are reversed rather than discarded, and both the
reversal and the original local score are recorded.

Maximising the satisfied weight is the minimum-weight feedback arc set
problem. Because the order is derived for the whole component, it can reverse
a relation that is not contradicted by any other; a reversal that lies on no
cycle only reduces the satisfied weight. When supplied with ground-truth crossings and their true
directions, which are acyclic by construction, the procedure still reverses 24
relations across the \PlectaDepthRuleHeldoutScenes{} evaluation scenes,
limiting layer agreement to $0.886$ instead of $1.000$. A variant that orders
the condensation of the strongly connected components and reorders only within
them reverses none of these relations, but performed worse on the actual,
noisy predictions and is not used in the released implementation.

\subsection{Layers and metric height}

The corrected relations form a directed acyclic graph, whose longest path
determines the smallest number of layers consistent with them. Strands that
are never in conflict share a layer, so the number of layers follows from the
ordering rather than from the number of instances. Each instance is then
assigned a height that respects the order and prevents interpenetration at the
measured radii, with the stack compacted to the minimum thickness these
constraints allow.
