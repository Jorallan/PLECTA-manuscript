### B. Figure 2 — panel (b) needs to be clearer

Panel (b) shows the tangent-reversal angle theta, measured between one stub's
outward tangent and the other's *reversed* tangent brought to a common origin.
The reversed tangent is drawn twice, once where it lives and once from the
common origin, which is where the angle is read. That double-drawing is the part
readers stumble on. Make the construction obvious at a glance.

### D. Figure 4 — unclear, especially panel (e)

Panel (e) shows a real three-arm node where the cheapest admissible edge is

### E. Figure 7 — remove panel (c), and decide the greedy baseline's fate

**Remove panel (c)** (the paired-difference forest plot). Its content is in the
comparator table.

**Then judge whether the greedy continuation baseline should stay at all.** The
author's objection is that it is not an external method, and that is fair. Weigh
these honestly:

- *Against keeping it*: it is our own reimplementation of a principle, not of
  any published method. The paper now has two genuinely external comparators

- *Against keeping it, more strongly*: the paper now contains a **matcher-only

- *For keeping it*: it is the rule SIFNE, GraFT's path cover and Wegmayr's
  continuity baseline all use, so it connects the claim to the literature; and

Make a recommendation with reasons. If it goes, it must go from the table, the

## 6. Two evidence additions, if space allows

Both are computed and verified; neither is in the manuscript, purely for space.

### Already measured — just needs writing in

GraFT's repository bundles **one 500x500 synthetic frame with per-filament
ground truth** (MIT licensed; the rasterisation was validated against their own
noise-free image at 1.000). Scored through the identical pipeline:

| | F1 | crossings resolved |
|---|---|---|
| PLECTA | **0.977** | **11 / 11** |
| greedy baseline | 0.912 | 9 / 11 |
| GraFT, own front end on the noisy image | 0.800 | 7 / 11 |
| GraFT, matched injection | 0.662 | 10 / 11 |
| connected-components floor | 0.192 | 0 / 11 |

This is **one frame** and carries no interval. It must not be presented as a
benchmark. What it establishes is that the comparison does not depend on our
generator, which is worth two sentences because same-generator evaluation is the
paper's principal limitation. The instructive detail: GraFT resolves 10 of 11
crossings correctly while scoring 0.662, so crossing accuracy and pairwise
agreement measure different failures.

### Worth running — scenes in the comparators' own regime

The strongest remaining answer to "is this method only good on its own
generator?" is to test it on ground chosen to favour the others.

**GraFT's generator is vendored** under `graft_comparison/vendor/GraFT` and the
output converter is already written and verified. Generating **about 50 scenes
in GraFT's own regime** — its filament densities, widths and curvatures, which
are sparser and less tangled than ours — was estimated at roughly half a day.
That is a genuine stress test biased toward the comparator.

If you do this: fix the protocol before running, keep seeds disjoint from
everything else, score through the same common-fragment path, and report it as
a *supplementary* regime rather than as the paper's main claim. Expect PLECTA's
margin to narrow, and report it honestly if it does — a narrower margin on the
comparator's home ground is a stronger paper than an unexplained large one.

**DNAi's data cannot be used**, and the reason should be recorded rather than
re-investigated: its bundled test image is 5.7 kB and unlabelled, its training
path is a private local directory, its Zenodo DOI is a software archive, and its
domain is two-colour DNA-fibre assays annotated by tract length rather than by
instance mask. There is no instance ground truth to score against.

## 7. What not to disturb

- The distinction in `sec:res-overlap` between **crossing resolution** (which
  arm continues into which — the metric does reward this, and it is why the
  comparators lose) and **crossing representation** (whether the raster marks a
  pixel as owned by two filaments — the metric cannot see this, because those
  pixels are discarded when fragments are built). An earlier draft wrongly said
  the metric ignores the paper's titular property. It does not. Do not
  reintroduce that error.
- The comparator caveats: each released method ran from its own source; a parity
  gate had to reproduce stored F1, ARI and both VI components before any
  comparator number was believed; every adaptation was checked and found to
  favour the comparator; GraFT's unfinished scenes are excluded from its means
  and that flatters it.
- The evidence boundaries, each stated once in the limitations subsection:
  same-generator held-out scenes are not distribution transfer; ablations are
  development-only; synthetic width recovery is not physical CNT diameter
  validation; manual-derived masks are an oracle-like input; the real evidence
  is two fields and only one is training-clean; comparator results do not
  establish a general ranking; the output is projected 2-D identity, not depth
  ordering.
- Two results that run against the method and are deliberately reported: PLECTA
  has the **steepest runtime scaling** of the three (log-log exponent 2.65
  against 2.11 and 2.01), and it **marks 3.6 to 4.0 times more shared pixels
  than the reference contains**, because junction blobs are painted into every
  chain that touches them.
